# Representative clip range

VISION step 4 asks the user to hold themselves to five to ten seconds of
footage while they tune. Until now `ClipRange` existed, `Project.clip` held it,
`plan.py` built a span from it and `sieve run` executed it — and nothing in the
GUI could produce one. `--frames` on the CLI was the only way to say which
seconds mattered.

Marking is where the playhead is: **I** and **O** under Playback, or the Mark
In / Mark Out buttons under the transport. The band is painted on a strip
directly beneath the slider and the span is written out beside it in seconds,
because a band a few hundred pixels wide cannot be read as a duration and the
duration is the number the user is being asked to keep small.

## What was checked by mutation

Four deliberate breakages, four distinct failures:

| mutation | tests that caught it |
| --- | --- |
| `end = frame` instead of `frame + 1` in `mark_clip_out` | 4 |
| the inversion guard dropped from `mark_clip_in` | 1 — a `ValueError` out of `ClipRange`, i.e. the crash the guard exists to prevent |
| the inversion guard dropped from `mark_clip_out` | 1, same shape |
| `x_of_frame` replaced with `groove.x() + fraction * groove.width()` | 4, including the midpoint |

The last one is the reason that test compares against `SC_SliderHandle`'s
centre rather than against `x_of_frame`'s own arithmetic: a test written the
obvious way is true by construction and survives every one of those mutations.

## What changed outside the item's scope

`_on_editor_open_changed` now disables the two mark actions alongside play and
delete. I and O are bare-letter window shortcuts, so without this a replicate
renamed to "Colony Six" marks a clip twice on the way through.

The clip readout label is `QSizePolicy.Ignored` horizontally. It is a sentence,
and a sentence in a layout sets that layout's minimum width — the
player/tool-pane splitter came up 133 px off centre until the label stopped
being allowed to vote on it. `test_the_player_and_the_tool_pane_get_equal_widths`
caught it, which is the second time that test has paid for itself.

The transport row is a `QGridLayout` rather than an `QHBoxLayout`, so the strip
can occupy the slider's column and only the slider's column. The band is drawn
in the slider's coordinates, so sharing the column is what keeps the two
aligned as the play button and timecode change width.
