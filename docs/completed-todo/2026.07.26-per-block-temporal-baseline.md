# Per-block temporal baseline

## What the item asked for and what it turned out to require

The item was a filter. It could not be written without changing the filter
contract, and that is the part worth recording, because the necessity was not
visible from the item's text.

`FilterSpec.warmup_frames` was a static integer. A filter whose lead-in is
`window_seconds × fps − 1` has to declare the product of both parameters' upper
bounds — 7200 frames — and `plan.py` would then decode 7199 frames of lead-in
before every run of any graph containing the node, including the run asking for
a 5 s window at 30 fps that needs 149. At roughly 15 ms/frame that is 108
seconds added to a cold render whose budget is 3. The filter as specified was
not implementable behind a static declaration, so the declaration moved:
`FilterSpec.warmup_frames` is now the **bound** and `ParamsBase.warmup_frames`
is the **need**, with `node_warmup_frames` picking between them.

The machinery cost almost nothing, which is itself the tell that it was overdue.
`input_warmup_frames` already received `(spec, params)` — the plan already had
every node's resolved parameters in hand at the moment it computed lead-in — so
the whole change is one function and one method. `background_ema` picked up the
refinement for free: its docstring had argued at length that decoding 83 unused
frames at `alpha = 0.5` was "the correct price of a declaration that is true for
every setting of every parameter", and that paragraph is now a historical note
in italics rather than a live argument. It is left standing rather than deleted
because the reasoning in it is still what forbids the refinement from ever
growing the bound.

## What was checked by mutation

Four deliberate breakages, and two of them found real problems rather than
confirming the tests:

- **`mean`/`std` in place of `median`/`MAD`.** Failed
  `test_events_do_not_inflate_the_spread_they_are_measured_against` (2.98 against
  a floor of 15) and also the bout test. Confirmed.
- **A constant `1e-3` spread floor in place of the borrowed spread.** *Survived
  the first version of the affine-invariance test*, because that fixture had no
  degenerate cell and so never engaged the floor at all — the test's own
  docstring claimed to catch exactly this and did not. The fixture gained a cell
  that is constant across its whole window; the mutation now fails it by 7x
  (3000 against 21000), which is the gain factor, which is the point.
- **`sample_stride` pinned to 1.** Failed
  `test_a_strided_ring_still_spans_the_whole_window` — a 900-frame window's
  baseline read 1071.5 where the window's midpoint is 749. Confirmed.
- **Handing out the cached baseline instead of a copy.** *Survived*, and
  correctly: `_estimate` allocates a fresh baseline on every admission, so there
  is nothing to alias today. The test asserting otherwise could not fail and was
  deleted rather than kept as decoration. The `.copy()` stays with a comment
  saying why it is insurance against an optimization nobody has made yet, and the
  test slot went to the strided-ring claim, which nothing had covered.

That second one is the reason this section exists. A test whose docstring names
the mutation it catches is not evidence that it catches it.

## What changed outside the item's scope

`background_ema`'s module docstring, the comment on its `warmup_frames=90`, and
the note on its `alpha` upper bound were all rewritten, because all three
described a static declaration that no longer exists.
`tests/unit/test_background_ema.py` gained one test asserting the refinement is
charged, so that dropping the override is a failure rather than a silent return
to 90 frames of lead-in.

`sieve inspect` gained one line. It prints `warmup_frames` bare, and bare it now
means two different things — for `temporal_baseline` it printed **7199**, which
reads as the lead-in every run decodes and would make the filter look unusable
to precisely the reader who came to find out whether it is. It now appends
"(worst case; each configuration is charged its own)", and only for the filters
that refine.

## What the next item inherits

**The motion history filter**, which is the last item in the temporal chain, is
the direct beneficiary in two ways. Its thresholds wanted units and now have
them. And its `tau_seconds` has exactly this filter's shape — a warmup that is a
parameter in physical units, needing an `fps` to convert — so the contract half
of that work is already done and the pattern to copy is
`TemporalBaselineParams.warmup_frames`.

What it does **not** inherit is a way to combine two signals. This filter is
single-upstream; the "high `change_energy`, low `coherence`" rule the coherence
item made expressible still has no node that evaluates it, and neither does
"deviation above k". Nothing in the temporal chain has yet needed a threshold
node, and it should arrive with the first thing that does rather than be guessed
at now.
