# The first live graph tick

## Why the item was split

As written, the item claimed both in-pipeline graph budgets were about to become
measurable. `filter_to_first_tick` was; `slider_to_graph` was not, and the gap
was not a matter of effort. Its label is "Slider drag → graph update", and
nothing in `gui/` edits a parameter — `ReplicateDocument.set_pipeline` exists and
every caller of it is a project load. Publishing that key from anything else
available would have meant timing a different gesture: a re-render triggered by
the working window moving decodes frames the store does not have, and the drag
the budget names is supposed to decode nothing at all. It is in `docs/LATER.md`
with a parameter panel as its trigger.

The other half of the split was size. Adapter, runner, and HUD is three modules,
and the risk is concentrated entirely in the middle one — a worker thread, a
cancellation with no upstream hook, and a coalescing rule. Shipping it with its
own tests, and pointing the HUD at a producer that already works, is a better
trade than making the HUD wait behind the part most likely to be wrong.

## The flag that could not be lowered

The first version shared a `threading.Event` between the GUI thread and the
render thread: set to cancel, cleared when the next render was issued. It is
wrong, and the way it is wrong is worth recording because the obvious fixes are
also wrong.

There is no safe moment to clear it. If the GUI thread clears it when it issues
the next render, it may do so before the worker has reached the frame boundary
where it would have seen the flag raised — so the superseded render never
notices and runs to completion while the new one waits behind it in the worker's
queue. If instead the *worker* clears it on entry to `render`, a request
superseded while it was still sitting in that queue has its cancellation
discarded before it starts, and runs in full for the same reason.

A number that each side only ever compares has no such moment. `_Wanted` holds
the newest revision; the GUI writes it on every submission and on every close,
and the worker asks once per frame whether the revision it is rendering is still
that one. Closing a video bumps the revision with nothing issued at it, so *no*
revision is current and the in-flight render abandons at its next frame — which
is the same stamp-not-a-flag argument `gui/coalescer.py` already makes about a
decode outliving the source it was asked for.

## What was checked by mutation

Deleting the two-line abandon check from `_RenderWorker.render`'s consumer —
the whole of the cancellation — leaves every signal the runner emits identical,
because the GUI side drops a stale revision's frames regardless. The suite
still fails:
`test_an_edit_mid_render_stops_the_render_it_replaced` asserts on kernel
invocations rather than on signals and reports `assert 24 < 24`, the superseded
render having computed all twelve of its frames before the replacement started.
That test is why the runner takes `registry` and `kernels`: `downsample` over
the 160x120 fixture finishes before a test can supersede it, so against the real
shelf this claim could only have been pinned by a race.

## What changed outside the item's scope

`MainWindow` gained a status-bar line reporting each render's frame count and
the store's reuse share, and reports a graph that will not run there rather than
in a dialog. It is a placeholder for the HUD and is called one in the code: with
nothing drawing the series yet, the alternative was a render path exercised only
by tests. The reuse share was chosen over a duration deliberately — the
durations are on the bus, and a second render reporting 0% reuse is the specific
failure `pipeline/preview.py` is written against and the one thing invisible in
the frames.

The preview's reader is capped at two decode threads rather than
`prefetch.py`'s inferred four. The player already owns a decode thread on the
same footage and the read-ahead holds full-resolution frames; a preview that
made scrubbing stutter would be buying an in-pipeline budget with a pre-pipeline
one, which non-negotiable #4 calls a defect rather than a tuning choice. The
number is a judgement, not a measurement — nobody has profiled the two pools
competing, and the day someone does is the day it should change.

The arena previewed is the first replicate, matching `sieve preview`'s default.
Following the replicate table's selection is the obvious next move and was left
out: it needs the arena named on the HUD's axis to mean anything to a user, and
that axis does not exist yet.
