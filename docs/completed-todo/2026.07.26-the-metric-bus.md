# ---- the load-bearing part ------------------------------------------------
decisions:
  - what: the clock is read before subscriber dispatch, not after
    why: the bus sits inside a 100 ms budget; otherwise every consumer measures itself
  - what: publish takes (key, elapsed_ms) against BUDGETS, never a gauge
    why: every budget is a duration, and the differencing arithmetic is what the keys exist to standardise
  - what: an unknown key raises KeyError
    why: a typo'd key is a metric that exists and is checked by nothing, which reads the same as one that passes
  - what: a block that raises publishes nothing
    why: a truncated interval improves the median, and that is the one error direction a latency gate cannot see
  - what: Sample carries the Budget, judged at publish
    why: one lookup on the way past instead of a copy of the table in every consumer
  - what: subscribers are a tuple swapped under a lock, iterated without one
    why: a subscribe from the GUI thread must not make a publisher wait inside a measured interval
  - what: gui/player.py publishes scrub_to_repaint
    why: a bus with no producer is the same non-commitment as a budget with no check, and the number was already being computed for ScrubPolicy

rejected:
  - what: named timer objects a caller starts and stops
    why: a timer outlives its interval and can be leaked, stopped twice, or stopped from the wrong thread; a context manager cannot
  - what: publishing ScrubPolicy's degradation verdict rather than the round trip
    why: that is the conclusion, not the measurement — a HUD and a gate both want the number
