# Decode the luma plane, because nothing reads chroma

The chain already discarded colour: `block_signal._gray` converts BGR to gray at
the extraction step, so every frame was decoded, downsampled and normalized in
three channels to produce a single-channel series. The convert and the 47.6 MB
buffer that carries it are 11.4 ms of a 19.4 ms frame.

What made this takeable rather than risky was measuring what it costs the answer
before building anything. The luma plane is *not* `cvtColor(BGR2GRAY)` — limited
range, and this footage is BT.709 where `cvtColor` weights BT.601, so the two are
not even related by an exact affine map. On the block series the detector
consumes, the difference is 0.004 sd mean and r = 0.99999. That number is why
this is a decode policy rather than a user-facing choice, and why it is hashed
rather than treated as transparent.

## What this did not fix

The render is 1.88x faster and playback during it is slightly *worse* — 23.8 to
19.6 fps on the colour viewport — because the player and the preview contend for
memory bandwidth, and a consumer that finishes sooner draws harder.
`docs/findings/2026.07.27-decode-is-a-bandwidth-wall-shared-by-two-consumers.md`
has the table.

The item was taken to make a frozen viewport less frozen, and it made the wait
shorter instead. The two follow-ups it leaves behind —
docs/todo/grayscale-viewport-toggle.md then docs/todo/render-fed-playback.md —
are what address the freeze itself, by removing a decode rather than cheapening
one.

## Checked by mutation

That the derived format actually reaches the key: inverting
`luma=not self.needs_chroma` in `Dag.node_keys` leaves every test passing except
`test_the_walk_is_the_hand_walk`, the one that re-derives the root key by hand.
That is why that test asks the graph rather than hard-coding `True` — pinned to a
literal it would have gone green on the mutation too.
