decisions:
  - what: >
      The SCAFFOLD call the item left open landed as `core/machine.py` — a
      shared home for both readings, with `available_cpus` moved there and
      `decode/prefetch.py` re-exporting it.
    why: >
      The memory resolver must be importable headless (CLI, HPC), which rules
      out `gui/`; and two machine-readings growing up in two modules is the
      two-callers-disagree failure `resolve_workers` documents, arriving as
      an OOM kill instead of a slow job. A reading is not a policy, so core's
      no-policy stance is not disturbed.
  - what: >
      `available_memory` consults `SLURM_MEM_PER_NODE`/`SLURM_MEM_PER_CPU`
      even though `resolve_workers` deliberately reads no environment.
    why: >
      The asymmetry is real, not an inconsistency: CPUs have affinity and a
      `--workers` flag, so the env var was a redundant third voice; memory
      has no affinity equivalent, no CLI surface on the GUI path, and the
      cost of guessing is a kill. The cgroup still outranks it, and PBS/LSF
      configurations without either fall through to physical as a named gap,
      not a claim.
  - what: >
      Shares are fractions of the post-reserve budget with declared floors;
      resolved size never drops below the floor, and the overcommit case is
      *reported* by `fits_memory`, never governed at runtime.
    why: >
      Fractions are what make one source file right on a 16 GB laptop and a
      256 GB node; floors keep the measured-era sizes as the minimum a
      consumer can do its job at. A cache resolved to zero would be a second
      failure mode, so the honest report (the `fits_machine` construction)
      carries the refusal instead.
  - what: >
      `resolve_worker_split` degrades detector first, then preview, never the
      player, and never scales any pool above the declared 1/2/2.
    why: >
      The detector's own docstring calls it the weakest claim and the
      `DETECTOR_WORKERS <= PREVIEW_WORKERS` test already pins the direction;
      scaling up on big machines is the exact mistake the 8- and 12-worker
      measurements made — the four-worker optimum is a bandwidth wall, not a
      core count.
  - what: >
      `MemoryFrameStore` holds no row; it is named in `UNBOUNDED`, the
      ledger's honest gap.
    why: >
      Bounding it is the eviction item's whole subject, and a fabricated row
      would make the sum read as complete while the largest consumer is
      missing — rule 6 in ledger form, same construction as
      `WITHOUT_PRODUCER`.

rejected:
  - what: A runtime governor, or a config surface for the budget.
    why: >

      longer has to carry. What to keep under the budget stays with the
      retention and eviction items.

    why: >

      they moved whole to docs/todo/ledger-measurements.md so the reserve and
      the worker cap stay visibly provisional instead of quietly settled.
