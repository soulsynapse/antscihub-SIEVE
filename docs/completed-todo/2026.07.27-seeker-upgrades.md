decisions:
  - what: >
      A handle drag is two new signals on the strip — `window_moved(origin)`
      and `window_resized(start, end)` — wired straight to two document verbs,
      rather than one signal carrying the resolved span.
    why: >
      A header drag holds the window's length and so *is* the edit
      `move_window_to` already performs; only a handle drag names both edges,
      and that is the one case needing the new `place_window`. Collapsing them
      would have made every window drag read "Resize Window" in the Edit menu,
      including the ones that resized nothing.
  - what: >
      The one-second floor lives in `timeline_model.started_at` /
      `ended_at_handle` and is enforced by the strip, not by the document.
    why: >
      `end_window_at` — the keyboard out-mark — is allowed to name a single
      frame, and always was. The floor is a property of a *drag*, where the
      cursor passes continuously through every shorter span on its way, not of
      the window. Putting it in the document would have silently changed what
      Mark Out means.
  - what: >
      A drag past the floor stops at the floor rather than being refused.
    why: >
      The item said "refused rather than committed", which is true of what
      reaches the document — nothing shorter than a second is ever written. A
      handle that went dead under a still-moving cursor would read as a broken
      widget; one that stops reads as a limit. This is also why the left handle
      uses `started_at` and not `ended_at`, whose release-the-origin rule is
      right for a keystroke that would otherwise do nothing and wrong for a
      grip the user is still holding.
  - what: >
      The bubble sits below the header band, not at the top of the track, and
      is not painted at all during a drag.
    why: >
      It follows the cursor, and the header is what the cursor is usually
      approaching; a readout that covers the handle it is guiding you to is
      worse than one a few pixels lower. During a drag the thing being dragged
      already answers "where am I".
  - what: >
      `STRIP_HEIGHT` stays at 44 px; the mockup's 64 is not adopted yet.
    why: >
      The 64 was sized for the detection ticks and coverage lanes, which have
      no producer (`docs/todo/coverage-and-detection-lanes.md`). The header
      band is nine pixels and fits inside 44 with room to spare, and twenty
      pixels of empty band is twenty pixels the graphs do not get.

rejected:
  - what: >
      Giving `SetClip` a `mergeWith`/`id()` so a per-move write collapses into
      one undo entry.
    why: >
      It makes the history correct only after the fact and makes every
      intermediate span a real document state — the player is bounded by the
      window, so each one would re-bound playback and re-render. The local
      draft is cheaper and is the two-tier rule the rest of this UI already
      follows for band drags.
  - what: >
      Re-classifying the grab on every mouse-move instead of once on press.
    why: >
      The window moves under the cursor during the drag, so a drag that
      re-asked what it was holding could change from a resize to a move
      halfway through, at the moment the opposite edge slid past the pointer.
