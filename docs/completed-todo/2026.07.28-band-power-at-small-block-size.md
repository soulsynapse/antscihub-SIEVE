      paths a value enters through — `stepBy` for wheel and arrows,
      `valueFromText` for typing.
  - what: >
      A value *already* below a newly-derived floor is left exactly where it is,

      pipeline holds. A project saved before the bound, or one whose crop grew
      under a fixed block size, is a real parameter value, and a widget that
      silently raised it would display a number the chain does not hold — rule
      6's mirror clause. `density_plot.set_series` is where every path into the

# Band power at small block size

The scatter half of this item was already gone when it was taken: `bin_counts`
and the identity cache landed with
`docs/findings/2026.07.27-the-density-histogram-was-a-scatter.md`, which is why
that finding is cited rather than re-derived. What remained was the 2026-07-27
decision on top of it — refuse at the control — plus the budget that makes the
threshold a measured ceiling, and the swallowed failure that made the symptom
read as "gives out" rather than "is slow".

The measurement is in
`docs/findings/2026.07.28-the-density-rebuild-buys-one-octave-below-auto.md`.
Its one surprise: the bound admits exactly one halving below the auto block
size on the full reference frame, and no arrangement of the current binning
admits two.

## What was checked by mutation

- Replacing `stepBy` with `super().stepBy` — the whole wheel path — fails all
  three tests in `TestTheRefusedRangeCannotBeSteppedInto` and *no* others. The
  typing path and the surface refusal are independently pinned; neither covers
  for the wheel, which is the gesture the item was reported against.
- Raising `MAX_BLOCKS` to 32,768 without re-measuring passes every test in
  `tests/gui/` and fails the benchmark. That is the right direction and worth
  recording, because the boundary test in `test_density_binning.py` follows the
  constant by construction and can never catch it moving — only the clock can.
  The two files are not redundant: the benchmark cannot say *which* B was
  refused, and the boundary test cannot say whether it was affordable.
- Freeing the runner's `_busy` flag on failure is load-bearing separately from
  reporting the failure: leaving it set passes the "a failure is reported" test
  and fails the "a later good pass still lands" one, which is the second
  symptom the user would actually have met.

## Outside the item's scope

`DensityPlot` grew a `notice` — the widget had no way to say anything, and the
refusal needs one. It follows `CountPlot`'s shape exactly (a string, painted
centred in the content rect) rather than inventing a second convention.
