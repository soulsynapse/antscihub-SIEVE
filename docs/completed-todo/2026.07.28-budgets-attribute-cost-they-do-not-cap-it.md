summary: >
  The user may ask for any block count and the app says what it costs: the
  density binning moved off the GUI thread into `density_surface`, computed on
  the detector thread and carried on `DetectorResult`; `MAX_BLOCKS` and the
  `block_spin` floor are deleted; and `GraphHud.attribution_line` names the
  leading span persistently, ranked by `elapsed / limit` rather than raw
  milliseconds. Landed over three commits — 0ed7e27, e394636, 9bfa648.

decisions:
  - what: "the `density_rebuild` producer moved with the work — `density_ms` is
      measured on the thread that does the binning, not around `set_series`"
    why: keeping the `perf_counter` at the widget would have published the
      QImage wrap, a number nothing waits on, and shown a met budget for work
      nobody timed — rule 6 with the sign flipped
  - what: "`attribution_line()` ranks by `elapsed / limit`, and `show_sample`
      keeps every key rather than only `WATCHED`"
    why: by wall clock the render wins every session (3000 ms ceiling against
      100), so a field ranked on milliseconds would always say "render" and
      attribute nothing
  - what: "`set_series(surface=)` is guarded by the same identity check that
      makes the cheap tier free"
    why: a surface handed for a *different* array would put one population's
      picture under another's axis, and the identity check is what makes
      accepting a precomputed surface safe at all
  - what: "block count 0 still refuses to be reached by an accelerated wheel
      run, stopping at 1"
    why: that boundary was never about performance — 0 is a *mode* (auto), and
      sliding into a mode change is a different gesture than choosing a number
  - what: "`min_block_for` and its test were deleted outright rather than kept
      unused"
    why: the floor was their only consumer and the arithmetic is in git if a
      bound ever returns; a live function computing a bound nothing applies is
      the next person's false premise
  - what: "the 100 ms `density_rebuild` ceiling was not moved"
    why: "with the binning off the GUI thread a miss means a graph filling late
      rather than a frozen window, so the ceiling may now be wrong — but moving
      a budget limit is a decision, not a cleanup. Left to Kendrick, and the
      residual timing debt repointed to
      docs/todo/budget-checks-under-ambient-load.md."

rejected:
  - what: raising `MAX_BLOCKS` to a larger number
    why: "the cap was a dev-workstation timing wearing a product parameter's
      clothes. Block count is a scientific choice about the grain of the
      analysis and the HPC target has neither this machine's clock nor this
      refusal's justification — a better wrong value is still a budget capping
      what the user may ask for."
  - what: taking step 2 (the cap comes off) before step 1 (the rebuild moves
      off the GUI thread)
    why: removing the refusal first trades a refusal for a frozen window, which
      is the worse half of rule 6
