decisions:
  - what: "`after:` is a new key and `gated_on` is untouched and unchanged in
      meaning"
    why: "they answer different questions — `after:` is \"which item must finish
      first\", `gated_on` is \"what real-world condition makes this timely\". A
      single key would have to be prose to hold both, which is the problem."
  - what: an edge pointing at a slug keeps working after that item completes
    why: "checked first, because everything rests on it —
      `completed-todo/2026.07.25-executor.md` yields `executor` under a
      `^\\d{4}\\.\\d{2}\\.\\d{2}-` strip, so no edge is rewritten when its
      target finishes"
  - what: blocked items are shown *with their blocker* rather than omitted from
      the frontier
    why: the frontier then partitions the open list instead of being a second
      shorter list a reader has to diff against the first
  - what: the mermaid block draws only edge-carrying nodes — 17 of 34
    why: "the item's own \"cut the graph rather than tune the layout\" applied
      to the axis that costs nothing: an item with no edge contributes nothing
      to a picture *of edges* and is already listed above in full"

rejected:
  - what: backfilling `serves:` across the item tree
    why: "the item predicted the honest outcome is that most items serve
      nothing in ASPIRATIONS.md and should say so by staying blank. Inventing
      edges to avoid showing a large body of work under no aspiration is the
      one failure mode it named."
  - what: designing `conflicts_with:` in the same pass
    why: the item says not to add a second edge kind before `after:` has been
      used and has shown what it costs
