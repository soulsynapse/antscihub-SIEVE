title: A filter never says what one element of its output means
date: 2026-07-28
commit: 48635fc
tags: []

summary: >
  A filter now declares what one value of its output is a value of, the graph
  resolves that declaration along every chain, and `sieve detect` names its CSV
  columns from the answer or refuses the node — so `--node` at a `downsample`
  node writes `pixels_in_band` where it used to write a pixel count labelled
  `blocks_in_band`, and a node that cannot be labelled writes nothing at all.

settled:
  - what: Element meaning is a filter declaration resolved along the graph
    where: "`src/sieve/core/filter_base.py`, `src/sieve/pipeline/dag.py`"
    do_not_redecide: >
      Not a field on `ArraySpec` and not a constant on every spec.
      `temporal_baseline` accepts any array and estimates per cell, so it emits
      blocks over `block_signal` and pixels over a raw frame — any constant it
      declared would be a lie in one of the two positions, which is why
      `ElementRelation.PRESERVED` exists and why the answer needs a walk.
      `node_element` is the one-node conversion and `Dag._elements` is the
      traversal that supplies its second argument, the same split
      `input_warmup_frames` and `plan._lead_in` have.
  - what: The declaration has no default, and an array emitter without one is refused
    where: "`src/sieve/core/filter_base.py` `FilterSpec.__post_init__`"
    do_not_redecide: >
      Defaulting `element` to `PRESERVED` costs nothing today — every filter on
      the shelf that preserves would be right by accident — and converts the
      next element-redefining filter's omission into a CSV column with an
      invented noun, which is the failure this whole item closed. `None` means
      "emits rows", enforced against `emits` in both directions so it cannot
      also mean "forgot". The price is that every `@register_filter` in the
      suite states it; that is the enforcement, not a side effect of it.
  - what: Aggregation is kind-dependent, and its open default fails closed
    where: "`src/sieve/core/filter_base.py` `node_element`"
    do_not_redecide: >
      `AGGREGATED` keeps `PIXEL` and refuses `BLOCK` — a mean of pixels is the
      scene sampled more coarsely, a mean of blocks is not a block. The branch
      names `PIXEL` and sends every other kind to `None`, which is a default
      over `ElementKind` in the opposite polarity to the one refused above: an
      `ElementKind` member added later is *refused* under aggregation until
      somebody decides what a mean of one means, rather than silently
      misnamed. Deliberate, not incidental.
  - what: A series node that cannot be labelled is refused, never renamed neutrally
    where: "`src/sieve/cli/detect_cmd.py` `_series_node`"
    do_not_redecide: >
      Three refusals, each a different wrong artifact: a table emitter (no
      per-frame series), a node behind a rate change (`frame = start + offset`
      is wrong by the rate, silently), and a node whose elements have no
      meaning. Renaming the columns to `units_in_band` instead was the
      alternative and is worse than either — honest and unreadable, which is
      the trade rule 6 exists to refuse rather than to make.
