summary: >
  The source's frame rate is now the container's own rational, probed off
  the stream header with PyAV: `VideoMetadata.fps` is a `Fraction`,
  `timestamp_of` and `duration_seconds` are `MediaTime`,
  `clip_window.default_window` counts its ten seconds through
  `FrameCount.spanning`, and the seconds columns in `detect/tables.py` are
  founded on the exact rate rather than `frame / float(fps)` — which at
  30000/1001 published frame 15 as a time that floors back to frame 14.

settled:
  - what: The source's frame rate is the container's rational, probed with PyAV
    where: "`src/sieve/decode/reader.py` `container_rate`, `src/sieve/core/types.py` `VideoMetadata.fps`"
    do_not_redecide: >
      OpenCV cannot answer it: `CAP_PROP_FPS` is a double and the
      denominator is gone by the time it returns. Recovering it with
      `Fraction(value).limit_denominator(...)` was the alternative and is
      right for the NTSC family by luck rather than by construction —
      silently wrong for any rate whose denominator exceeds the limit, which
      is the worst kind of number to found a published timestamp on. Zero
      stays the honest refusal for a container that declares no rate. The
      probe reads the header and never a frame, which is what keeps PyAV
      from becoming a second decoder identity (`pyproject.toml`).
  - what: The three filters' `fps` params stay `float`, and the two representations meet in one place
    where: "`src/sieve/gui/filter_tab.py` `_fps`"
    do_not_redecide: >
      A params field is hashed into `canonical_json`, so retyping
      `block_signal`, `motion_history` and `temporal_baseline`'s `fps` would
      re-key every entry those filters ever cached — for kernels that
      multiply a window by a number and want nothing exact.
      `float(Fraction(30000, 1001))` is the same double `CAP_PROP_FPS` used
      to hand over, so the conversion moves no key;
      `tests/integration/test_decode.py` asserts that against OpenCV rather
      than assuming it.
  - what: A published second is computed in exact arithmetic, and refuses when the rate is absent
    where: "`src/sieve/detect/tables.py` `_at_frame`, `_seconds`"
    do_not_redecide: >
      The CSV is what somebody takes into R and lines a video up against, so
      `frame / float(fps)` naming the wrong frame is the failure the whole
      item exists to close. `NA` rather than `0.000` from a source that
      declares no rate (rule 6), which also removes the `ZeroDivisionError`
      that path used to raise. Rounding is half-to-even on the `Fraction`
      and not on a double, because the ties are reachable: frame 15 at
      30000/1001 is 0.5005 s exactly, and which side a double falls on is a
      property of the approximation rather than of the number.
