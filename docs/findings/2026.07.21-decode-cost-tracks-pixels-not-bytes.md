---
title: Decode cost tracks pixels, not bytes
measured: 2026-07-21
where: antscihub-optical-flow-detector, core/pretranscode.py
status: not yet re-measured in this tree
---

Cutting a source once into small per-replicate clips made later decodes roughly
25x cheaper, and the size of the *file* had almost nothing to do with it. This
is the finding the tuning loop's feasibility rests on, and it is recorded here
rather than argued in an ADR because it is a measurement: it was taken on one
machine, one build and one piece of footage, and the right response to
disagreeing with it is to take another beside it.

## What was measured

`antscihub-optical-flow-detector`'s `core/pretranscode.py`, on `GX010047c2`
(5312x2988 HEVC), 300 frames of one replicate:

| route                         | time   | speedup |
| ----------------------------- | ------ | ------- |
| source HEVC, cropped on decode | 4.03 s | 1.0x    |
| ffv1 lossless clip             | 0.16 s | 25.4x   |

A lossy CRF 18 clip of the same region decoded at 28.6x — 12% better than
lossless, against a much smaller file. That gap is the finding: the stored bytes
fell by an order of magnitude more than the decode time did, so what the decoder
is paying for is pixels reconstructed, not bits read.

## Why the crop did not help

H.264 and HEVC decode whole frames. A crop expressed in the filter graph runs
*after* reconstruction, so a pass keeping the ~8% of the frame the replicates
occupy still pays for all 15.9 M pixels. Making the stored file small is the
only way to make a later decode genuinely decode fewer pixels, and that has to
happen once, ahead of the loop, rather than per pass.

## The consequence worth carrying

There is no fidelity-versus-speed trade to agonise over at the cut, because the
lossy route buys 12% and costs quality. Take the lossless encode and pay in
storage. The trade that *is* real is storage against inherited noise: the same
module measured lossless clips at 131% of the source, because an already
re-encoded source's compression noise must be encoded verbatim and noise does
not compress, against 24% at CRF 12. It settled on a middle quality as the
default and recorded the choice in its manifest rather than fixing it, because
squared frame differencing is perturbed by inter-frame coding specifically —
so clips cut at different quality must not compare as equal.

## What this does not say

Nothing here has been re-measured in this tree, which has no decoder. The
numbers are the shape to expect and the reason to build the cut before building
around the absence of one; they are not a budget. `origin/v3`'s
`decode/prefetch.py` holds the neighbouring measurement — that threading whole
reads stops paying past a handful of workers, because each read allocates and
frees a full-resolution frame and the page faults serialise — and reaches the
same consequence from the other side: the win is in not materialising
full-resolution frames at all.
