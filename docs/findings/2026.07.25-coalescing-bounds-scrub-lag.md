# Coalescing bounds scrub lag

This finding is about what a latency budget on a scrub can honestly mean.

One random seek is ~80 ms on this footage and the budget at the time was 50 ms.
Read literally, that is a permanent miss with no fix. But the number a user
perceives during a drag is not the cost of one decode — it is how far the
viewport trails the cursor, and those diverge the moment requests are allowed to
queue. Forty queued seeks put the last one 3.2 seconds behind the mouse and
render 38 frames the user had already scrubbed past.

So the player holds at most one decode in flight and one pending, and the
pending slot is overwritten rather than appended to. Everything in between is
discarded. Measured, the 40-seek burst settled in 172 ms: two decodes' worth,
which is the floor for "the one in flight cannot be cancelled, then do the one
that matters".

That result is the reason `scrub_settle` later exists as a separate budget. The
quantity this measurement actually bounds is the *release*, not the drag, and
splitting them was overdue.

**What did not survive.** The framing here — "no amount of tuning fixes that for
5.3K H.264" — was asserted from one seek measurement, not from probing the
alternatives. Proxy decode, keyframe-only scrubbing and hardware decode were
left standing as untested escape hatches. They were subsequently measured; see
`2026.07.25-the-seek-is-irreducible.md`, which is also where the 50 ms budget
this entry is written against became 100 ms.
