# Decode cost is colour conversion

The ratio is what matters here, not the absolute numbers: 1.3 ms to demux and
decode a 5.3K H.264 frame against 29 ms to turn it into BGR. Nothing about the
codec is the bottleneck. The pixel format conversion is, and it scales with
frame area rather than with bitrate — which is why the same measurement on
1080p footage would show a completely different balance and must not be assumed
to transfer.

Two decisions fall straight out of it.

**Short forward jumps grab.** At 1.3 ms per grabbed frame, walking forward
through a 40-frame gap costs ~52 ms against ~80 ms for a seek, and it lands on
the exact frame instead of wherever the container's index rounds to.
`GRAB_FORWARD_LIMIT` is set at that crossover. Above it, seeking wins.

**Real-time playback is refused rather than approximated.** 34 fps of decode
against a 59.94 fps source is not a tuning problem. The alternative to dropping
frames is playing every frame at 57% speed, which for footage being judged on
behaviour timing is the wrong artifact entirely — a slow-motion clip that does
not announce itself as slow motion. So the player drives from elapsed wall time
and drops what it could not decode.

The verification is the part worth keeping: 2 s of playback advanced 117 source
frames. That is 58.5 source-fps against a 59.94 fps source — within 2.4% of real
time — while only 36.5 frames per second were actually rendered. Speed correct,
frame rate lower, which is what an NLE does.
