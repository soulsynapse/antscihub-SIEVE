# The seek is irreducible

Two of the three hatches are shut outright. Hardware acceleration does not
engage in `opencv-python-headless` — this is not inferred from timing, the
backend reports `HW_ACCELERATION = 0.0` — and dropping the colour conversion
buys 9% at the cost of getting back an `8UC1` buffer that cannot be shown to
anyone. **The seek itself is ~70% of the round trip and exposes no knob.**

The keyframe probe is the one worth reading carefully, because it is a negative
result and a negative result with no stated prediction is indistinguishable from
not having looked.

If the cost of `set(POS_FRAMES)` were what its name suggests — seek to the
preceding keyframe, then decode forward through the GOP to the requested frame —
then sweeping the target across 150 consecutive frames would trace a sawtooth:
cheap on each keyframe, rising linearly until the next one, dropping again. That
is the shape the hypothesis demands. There is no sawtooth. Cost ranges 43–124 ms
with no periodicity at any offset tested. So aligning a request to a keyframe
cannot be implemented as a cheaper seek, because keyframe distance is not what
the cost is made of.

What "keyframe seek" *can* mean is fewer seeks. That is the version that got
built, and it is why the user-facing notice says **coarse seek** rather than
keyframe seek — the wording follows the mechanism, and the mechanism is
repetition, not alignment.

So the budget moved to 100 ms and is now enforced by degrading. `ScrubPolicy`
watches the median of the last 5 scrub round trips; above budget it snaps drag
targets to a 1-second grid and `FrameCache` serves the repeats. The first pass
over a region costs exactly what it did before. The grid is small and stable, so
every later visit is a cache hit — and a cache hit does not seek, which is the
entire point. Releasing the slider always decodes the exact frame, so coarse
mode costs accuracy only while the mouse is down.

The reference machine does *not* degrade on this clip: 68 ms median is inside
the 100 ms budget. That is the correct outcome, and it is also why the
degradation tests inject a policy with a threshold the machine can actually
cross — waiting for the real budget to be missed would mean the test only runs
on the machines where it is least needed.

**Still open.** A sparse pre-decoded thumbnail track is what an NLE actually
does, and it is what would make the *first* pass over a region cheap too. Not
built: it needs somewhere to put the thumbnails, which is the same unmade
decision as where a project file lives.
