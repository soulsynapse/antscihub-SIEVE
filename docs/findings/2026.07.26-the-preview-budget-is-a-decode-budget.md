# The preview budgets are decode budgets until the store is warm

The two numbers to hold next to each other are **1350.2 ms** and **3.3 ms**: the
same 60-frame window, the same two-node graph, one parameter changed between
them. The second is not a faster render of the first. It is the render that did
not decode, because every entry above the edited node was still in the store and
`execute` only touches the reader when a root misses.

That factor of 409 is the whole justification for
`pipeline/preview.py` being a module. A preview loop written in the GUI would
almost certainly have rebuilt its store per render — it is the obvious thing to
do when the graph changes — and it would have produced identical frames while
turning every slider release into a 1.4 s wait on a one-second clip and a 13 s
wait on the ten-second clip VISION step 4 actually asks for.

## Why the 3 s ceiling cannot be met cold, in one line of arithmetic

Cold cost divided by frames of `decode_range` is 22.5 ms (60 frames) and 21.9 ms
(150 frames). Two graphs with different filters and a 2.5x difference in frame
count agreeing to within 3% is what says the cost is per *decoded frame* and not
per node: 3000 ms buys about 137 frames. At 59.94 fps that is 2.3 seconds of
footage, against a budget whose own label says "5–10 s clip" — 300 to 600
frames.

So the label and the number disagree, and they have disagreed since the table
was written; nothing could notice until something published the key. The number
is not wrong for the thing it governs, which is the render a user waits for after
an edit — that one takes 3.3 ms. It is wrong as a statement about the first
render of a window, and the first render of a window is a different event that
has no key.

## The stateful case is the same claim with the lead-in added

`background_ema` declares 90 warmup frames and is uncacheable
(`2026.07.26-stateful-output-is-not-keyed-by-what-it-is.md`), which costs twice:

- **Cold**, `decode_range` is 150 rather than 60 frames, so the first render is
  3281.8 ms and misses the ceiling by 282 ms where the stateless chain met it
  with 1.6 s to spare. The extra 1.9 s is 90 decodes, not 90 EMA steps.
- **Warm**, its 150 kernel calls cannot be served from the store, so every render
  pays them: 24.2 ms against the stateless chain's 3.3 ms. Still 124x inside
  budget, and it will not stay that way — this arena is 900x700 downsampled by 4,
  and the EMA is a handful of numpy ops over 39k pixels.

The second cost is the one to remember, because it is the one that grows: a
stateful node's lead-in is re-run on *every* render forever, so the frames above
it are the ones that most want a materialized checkpoint.

## What would flip the conclusion

A decode that skipped the full-frame YUV→BGR convert. The per-frame cost measured
here (~22 ms) is within noise of the 29 ms `retrieve()` already isolated in
`2026.07.25-decode-cost-is-colour-conversion.md`, and the ROI in this probe is
900x700 — 4% of the frame area that was converted to produce it. A reader that
converted the crop rather than the frame, or read a materialized crop, would move
the cold number by an order of magnitude and make the 3 s ceiling a statement
about a real 10-second window. That is `pipeline/materialize.py` and
`decode/reader.py`, not this module, which is why this finding is `open` rather
than `closed`: the consequence has been named and not acted on.
