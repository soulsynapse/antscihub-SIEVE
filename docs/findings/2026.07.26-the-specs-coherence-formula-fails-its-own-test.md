# The spec's coherence formula fails its own test

The derivation is three lines and worth keeping, because it says the failure
is structural rather than a matter of thresholds. For an isotropic texture
(gradient energy `s` per axis, cross-terms averaging out) translating at
`(u, v)` px/frame, brightness constancy gives `It = -(u·Ix + v·Iy)`, so every
space-time gradient is orthogonal to `(u, v, 1)` and the block tensor's
eigenvalues are

    λ1 = s(1 + u² + v²),   λ2 = s,   λ3 = 0.

The drafted scalar reads the *top* pair: `((λ1-λ2)/(λ1+λ2))² =
((u²+v²)/(2+u²+v²))²` — 0.11 at one pixel per frame, speed-dependent, and
never near 1 at realistic speeds. The signature of coherent translation is
not a gap between λ1 and λ2 (for a 2-D texture both are spatial energy); it
is the *null direction* — λ3 ≈ 0 against a nonzero λ2. That is exactly what
the spatial coherency measure from the tensor-based motion literature
(Haussecker & Spies, in Jähne's *Handbook of Computer Vision and
Applications*) reads: `((λ2-λ3)/(λ2+λ3))²`. The drafted formula is Bigün's
2-D coherence transplanted to 3-D with the eigenvalue indices kept — in 2-D
the smaller eigenvalue *is* the residual, in 3-D it no longer is.

The spec named the test as the arbiter ("a synthetic translating texture must
score near 1 and a synthetic in-place flicker near 0"), so the formula was
treated as the draft and the test as the contract.

Two boundary behaviours the numbers above justify writing down:

- **Strong flicker is required for the ~0 pole.** Near-static blocks read
  *high* under spatial coherency — with negligible temporal change, λ3 is the
  (tiny) temporal eigenvalue and the vacuous claim "one translation explains
  this" is arithmetically true. Exactly-zero change is gated to 0 in the
  kernel; the near-zero case is documented as "coherence conditions
  change_energy, it does not detect alone".
- **The ordering test needs opposing motion, not translation.** A swapped
  implementation (decompose per pixel, then reduce) still scores uniform
  translation 0.87 — locally everything is coherent. Only a block containing
  *mixed* directions separates the orders: 0.010 correct vs 0.496 swapped,
  because averaging per-pixel verdicts destroys the anisotropy the block
  aggregation exists to measure.
