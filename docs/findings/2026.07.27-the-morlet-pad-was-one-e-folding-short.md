# The Morlet pad was one e-folding short

## What the docstring claimed and what the arithmetic did

`morlet_power` said it "zero-pads past the largest wavelet's e-folding support
so the ends see zeros instead of circularly wrapping onto the other end of the
record". It computed `support = ceil(max(coi_efolding_s) / dt)` — one e-folding
time — and then `next_fast_len` rounded the total up.

One e-folding is not "past the support". It is the distance at which the
Gaussian envelope has fallen to `1/e`, so 37% of the wavelet's amplitude is
still there. `coi_efolding_s`'s own docstring says as much, in as many words:
*"This is a scale, not a threshold: contamination decays through the wedge
rather than stopping at its edge."* The padding code was the one caller that
read it as a threshold.

The gap that actually resulted was smaller still, because `next_fast_len` is
free to round to barely more than the request: 56 frames at T=200, 62 at T=400,
against a 0.5 Hz wavelet whose meaningful support runs past 110.

## The prediction that made the negative result readable

If the wrap is real, energy placed hard against the *end* of a record must
appear at its *beginning*, and the effect must shrink geometrically as padding
grows — because the envelope is Gaussian in the exponent, not linear.

Both held. A burst at frames 170-200 of a 200-frame record put 2.4% of full
band-power scale into frames 0-60, and each extra e-folding of padding cut it by
roughly two orders of magnitude until it hit a floor at ~1.7e-7 (float32, and
independent of padding from four onward). Three is the first multiple at the
floor, which is why it was chosen rather than two: two is already good at
1.5e-5, but three costs only a few hundred more samples in one FFT and removes
the term from consideration entirely.

## Why this was invisible until the graphs started filling

Nothing was wrong with the whole-clip path in a way anyone would have noticed.
The contamination needs strong signal near a record boundary, and it lands at
the opposite boundary — which on a long clip is a small fraction of the area
and, in a scalogram, is drawn inside the COI fade that already says "do not
read this". The detector's gate, though, does not fade. It thresholds.

The partial-derivation path made it constant rather than occasional: every
partial pass cuts the record at the newest frame, so whatever the render is
currently decoding sits *permanently* against a boundary. What had been a rare
coincidence became the steady state, and it surfaced as a gate that lit frames
0-26 on one pass and dropped them on the next.

## The second number, which is not the same number

`PAD_EFOLDINGS` stops the record wrapping. It does not make a truncated
record's trailing values final — those are still contaminated by the pad that
is legitimately there, and they still change when real frames replace it.

That is `COI_SETTLE_EFOLDINGS`, and it had to be measured *after* the padding
fix, because before it the wraparound term dominated and made the trailing
error look flat with respect to the frontier. Once padding was sufficient the
shape appeared: at one e-folding the worst error inside the claimed-settled
region sits exactly at the last settled index — the trailing pad, still
leaking, at ~1.5e-3 of scale. At two it drops to ~4e-5 *and the worst index
moves into the interior*, where it is FFT-length discretization between two
record lengths rather than the cut at all. Three changes nothing further.

The argmax moving is the load-bearing observation, not the magnitude. It is
what distinguishes "the frontier is still the limiting factor" from "something
else now is", and it is the reason two is defensible rather than merely
better-than-one.
