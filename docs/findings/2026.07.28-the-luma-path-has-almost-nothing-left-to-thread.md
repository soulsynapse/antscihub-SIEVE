# The luma path has almost nothing left to thread

```
                        luma          colour (same session)
sequential              8.49 ms       20.02 ms
workers=1               8.44          20.44
workers=2               6.52  <- opt  14.37
workers=3               6.93          13.86  <- opt
workers=4               7.88          14.44
workers=5               9.00           —
workers=6              10.63          19.30
workers=8              14.12          20.84
workers=12             20.04          24.97
                       1.30x          1.44x
```

The prediction this was run to test was that the optimum would move **up**. The
threading finding attributed the four-worker peak to a 47.6 MB BGR allocation
per frame saturating memory bandwidth; the luma plane is 15.9 MB, so a third of
the traffic ought to admit two or three times the workers before the same wall.

It went the other way. Two workers, and by five the pool is slower than not
having one.

## Why down is the right answer and the prediction was the wrong model

The colour path's 20 ms is one cheap thing and one expensive one: ~1.3 ms of
demux and decode, and the rest a single-threaded YUV420 to BGR24 convert
performed on the calling thread. Threading whole `read` calls works there
because the expensive part is per-call and embarrassingly parallel — N threads
convert N frames at once, and the ceiling is where their allocations collide.

`luma=True` does not parallelise that convert. It **deletes** it. What is left
per frame is the decode plus a copy of one 15.9 MB plane, and that is 8.5 ms
rather than 20. Threading it can only overlap what remains, and what remains is
much smaller — so the win is smaller (1.30x against 1.44x) and the count at
which the remaining work is covered is lower (two against three or four). The
buffer got smaller *and so did the job*; only the first half of that was in the
prediction.

The degradation past four is the same story from the other end and is if
anything sharper here: at twelve workers the luma path is 20.04 ms, which is
2.4x the sequential reader and almost exactly what the colour path costs
sequentially. Whatever serialises — page faults on repeated large allocations,
per-worker demux of a stream each reader is walking independently — it is paid
in full while there is now very little convert to hide it behind.

## The one number that is not tight, and why the conclusion survives it

Three end-to-end runs of the luma sweep disagree at the optimum. Runs two and
three agree closely (6.41 and 6.52 at two workers, 6.93 and 6.64 at three); the
first run measured 8.65 at two workers — no win at all — and put its own best at
three. The sequential reader was stable across all three (8.12 / 8.49 / 8.53),
so the variance is in the pool rather than in the machine's decode rate. It was
not chased down.

The conclusion does not rest on it. **In all three runs, four workers was worse
than that run's optimum**, and the optimum was two or three. That is the claim
the constant encodes, and it is what makes inferring four a regression rather
than a wash. Between two and three, two wins the median and two of three runs,
holds a third less in-flight memory, and is already what `PREVIEW_WORKERS`
declares — three would have been defensible on run one alone and is the runner-up
to revisit if this is ever re-measured.

## Why the colour anchor was taken at all

Because the earlier curve was measured in a different session and cannot be
trusted as a baseline for this one: the same sequential BGR probe reads 20.02 ms
here against 24.50 there, an 18% difference on the same machine and the same
file with nothing between them but two days. The *shape* reproduced exactly —
peak at three or four, degradation from six, worse-than-sequential by twelve —
which is what licenses comparing the two curves at all. Had the luma sweep been
run alone, its 1.30x could have been read against 1.61x as a much larger collapse
than it is.

## What would flip this

A reusable output buffer. The threading finding named `VideoCapture::read` into
a preallocated `Mat` as the probe that would separate allocation churn from
bandwidth, and it is more attractive now, not less: if the luma pool's ceiling
is allocation, a ring of reusable planes should push the optimum back up and buy
more than 1.30x. If it does not move, then two workers is what a 15.9 MB plane
costs and both caps are properties of the frame size rather than of the API.
