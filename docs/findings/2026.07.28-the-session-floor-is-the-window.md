# The session has no floor, it has a window

H3 asked for the session's RSS floor: app open, video loaded, nothing
rendered. The premise is that such a state has a characteristic size. It does
not.

## The two idle stretches

The same session, untouched, twice:

| stretch | working window | behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| first ~95 s after load | large (as loaded) | 3388 → 4717 MB, ~14 MB/s, then released |
| last ~330 s | shrunk | flat 1614–1709 MB, no drift |

An idle app that climbs 1.3 GB and an idle app that is flat for five and a
half minutes are the same build, the same footage, the same machine, minutes
apart. The only difference is the window.

That also disposes of the leak hypothesis. A leak does not stop, and this
stops — decisively, for 330 s. What fills is bounded by the extent of the
working window, which is exactly the signature of `pipeline/cache.py`'s
`MemoryFrameStore`, the sole entry in `gui/concurrency.py`'s `UNBOUNDED` and
the one consumer with no declared ceiling.

**The peak was reached while idle, not while tuning.** During the actual
work — dragging, materialising, dragging again — the session sat at ~1.65 GB,
about a third of its idle peak. Whatever the ledger is protecting against, it
is not the user working.

## Why this makes `memory_reserve` wrong in the direction that matters

The reserve is `min(4 GB, max(2 GB, 25%))` — a function of total RAM. The
things it reserves for are Python, Qt, the driver, font caches, decoder
buffers, and (on this evidence) an unbounded window-scoped cache. None of
those scale with how much RAM the machine has. One of them scales with the
window, which the user sets.

Read across machines, holding the measured behaviour fixed:

| allocation | reserve | large-window idle demand | verdict |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 68 GB | 4.29 GB | ~4.7 GB | marginal — peak exceeds reserve, total still fits |
| 16 GB | 4.00 GB | ~4.7 GB | reserve exceeded; declared consumers divide a budget partly already spent |
| 8 GB | 2.15 GB | ~4.7 GB | reserve exceeded by more than 2× |

The formula is least wrong on the largest machine and worst on the smallest,
because the fraction term grows the reserve exactly where it is least needed
and shrinks it exactly where the fixed costs dominate. The `max(2 GB, ...)`
lower bound is the suspect term, and it is suspect precisely on the hardware
nobody measured.

This is a prediction, not a result. The demand column is this machine's
behaviour transplanted, and a smaller machine may well behave differently —
a smaller proxy cache, a smaller window by necessity, more aggressive OS
pressure. But the prediction is falsifiable in one 30-second reading on any
8 GB machine, and that reading is now the highest-value measurement available
for the ledger.

## The part that generalises past the GUI

`MemoryFrameStore` being bounded only by window extent is not an interactive
problem that happens to show up in a GUI. It is a property of
`pipeline/cache.py`, which the CLI and any headless or cluster run also use.
`gui/concurrency.py` deliberately holds no policy for `core/` — a whole-clip
pass on a node "has nobody to leave room for" — and that reasoning is sound
for *sharing*, but it says nothing about an absolute ceiling. A node with 8 GB
per job step running a long clip has the same unbounded store and no GUI
window to incidentally bound it.

So the bound `docs/todo/cache-eviction.md` will add should be expressed
against the store, not against the session, or the fix will land in the one
consumer that already had the least trouble.

## What H4 can and cannot say

Peak 4.72 GB against declared (1.15 GB) plus reserve (4.29 GB) = 5.44 GB. No
gross overrun.

That is the entire answer, and it is thinner than it looks. Splitting the
4.72 GB into declared-versus-undeclared is impossible, because nothing
publishes what the four declared consumers were actually holding. The ledger
asserts 1.15 GB; no instrument confirms it. So H4 answers "did the total blow
out" — no — and cannot answer the question it was written for, which is
whether the ledger *describes* what the process holds.

That is not a shortcoming of this session. It is `docs/todo/ledger-producers.md`
stated as a measurement: the ledger cannot be audited from outside the
process, and nothing inside it reports.
