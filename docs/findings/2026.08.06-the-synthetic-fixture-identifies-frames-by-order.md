# The synthetic fixture identifies frames by order, not by the value it wrote

The fixture's purpose is stated in its own docstring: a test can assert *which*
frame a seek landed on. That purpose survives — the frames are strictly ordered
and separated by 4 to 7 levels, which is unambiguous. What does not survive is
the literal reading, and the literal reading is what a new test reaches for
first. The obvious assertion, `decoded ≈ n * 5` within half a step, fails at 4.0
on the very first run.

The error is not noise. Every frame after the first reads low, mean deficit
-2.5, and the field stays exactly uniform — peak-to-peak zero — so this is mp4v
quantising a whole flat frame downward rather than dithering it. That
uniformity is worth as much as the ordering: it means a test may sample one
pixel and a test may take the mean and the two cannot disagree about which
frame they are looking at, which is the failure mode a nonuniform fixture would
have introduced silently.

What a passing result would have looked like: decoded means within a level or
so of `n * 5`, error centred on zero. Neither holds.
