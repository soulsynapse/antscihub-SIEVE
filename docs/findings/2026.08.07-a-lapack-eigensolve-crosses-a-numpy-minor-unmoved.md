# A LAPACK eigensolve and a Gaussian blur cross a numpy minor and an OpenCV minor unmoved

The prediction worth writing down is the one that failed. `np.linalg.eigvalsh`
does not compute eigenvalues, it calls a LAPACK driver, and which driver — and
in what blocking — is a decision the numpy build makes rather than the caller.
A minor version that switched drivers, or a BLAS built with different
vectorisation, would change the last bits of `lam2` and `lam3`; the coherence
scalar is `((lam2 - lam3) / (lam2 + lam3))**2`, which is a ratio of a
*difference* of two nearly equal numbers, so it is the arrangement that
amplifies such a change rather than absorbing it. On a block where the tensor
is close to rank-deficient — which is every block of the translating fixture,
by construction, since that is what coherence near 1 means — `lam3` is small
and its relative error is large. That is the case designed to break, and it did
not.

What would refute this: a single unequal element in any of the four arrays,
which would mean the exact-equality rule is a fact about how light 04.3's
arithmetic was rather than a property of the mechanism. It is left `open`
because nothing was changed on the strength of it — it is evidence for a rule
already in force, and the value is that the rule is now known to hold one tier
further up than it had been tested.

The other half of 04.3's finding — that a golden's *coverage* is the fixture's
and not the mechanism's — is untouched here and still applies. Four goldens on
one frame pair pin four code paths at one point each. `block_signal`'s colour
conversion and its shape-change refusal are reached by none of them, which is
why `docs/todo/block-signal-refuses-and-converts-with-no-case.md` exists.
