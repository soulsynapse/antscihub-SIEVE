# A lookahead at the end of a video is a decode error, not a shortfall

`plan.py`'s asymmetry is deliberate and argued: frame zero is the floor on every
machine, so `decode_start` clamps and reports `lead_in_shortfall`, while how many
frames the footage holds is a fact about a container the module may not open, so
the trailing end is asked for and the reader supplies what exists.

What the reader actually does with "supply what exists" is refuse. The guard is
one line above the codec and it is the right guard for a random-access read —
frame 4000 of a 3000-frame video is a caller's mistake, not an empty result — but
the executor is not that caller. It is asking for frames it was told to ask for
by arithmetic that deliberately declined to know the length, and the answer it
gets is an exception rather than a short run.

So the failure at the end of a video is loud rather than silent, which is the
better half of the surprise: nothing is computed from a window that ran off the
end and reported success. What it costs instead is that a span ending at the last
frame of the footage cannot be run at all once anything in the graph declares a
lookahead — the mirror of `decode_start`'s "refusing to run would make the
opening seconds of every video untunable", with the refusal actually happening.

No tool declares a lookahead yet, so nothing is broken today; 04.8's centred
detector is what makes this reachable, and the pool item is where the choice
between clamping, reporting, and refusing gets made.
