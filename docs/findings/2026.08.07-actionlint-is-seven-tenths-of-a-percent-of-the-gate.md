# actionlint is seven tenths of a percent of the gate

The measurement was designed to price one objection and it killed a different
one. 0.7% does not decide anything on its own — a check that could only ever
fail on a `.github/`-only commit would be worth arguing about at any price,
because the argument is about what the gate line *means*, not what it costs.
What settles it is the pair of mutations. Both are things a person actually
types, both are invisible to YAML parsing, and both are fatal in the specific
way that removes the job: there is no runner for `ubuntu-latests` and no valid
step where `used:` is. The reading where actionlint lives inside `ci.yml`
therefore does not catch these one push late. It never catches them.

That leaves the gate line and a `paths:` filter, and between those the cost is
the whole difference — 0.16 s against a second enumeration of which checks
apply to which files, which is the shape 00.3 exists to refuse. A number an
order of magnitude larger, say two seconds, would have made the filter worth
its second list; 0.16 s is not near that.

The shellcheck line is the finding's own loose end. actionlint shells out to
shellcheck for every `run:` block and to pyflakes for every `python -c`, and
silently drops both rules when the binaries are absent. Locally they are
absent, so the 0.16 s is the price of the reduced rule set, and both the timing
and the two reds above are measured with those rules off. Neither mutation is
in a `run:` block, so neither verdict depends on them.
