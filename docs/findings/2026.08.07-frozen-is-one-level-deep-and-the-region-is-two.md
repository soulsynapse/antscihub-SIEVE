# Frozen is one level deep and the region is two

`Replicate.override_for` copies with `dict(...)`, `with_override` rebuilds with
`{key: dict(value) for ...}`, and `resolved_params` merges with `{**a, **b}`.
All three are one level deep, and all three are correct for the parameter shape
v2's replicate deviation actually carried: scalars — a threshold, a blur
radius. The case that breaks them arrived with this item. Under
`adr/detector-is-a-node.md` a replicate's geometry stopped being
`Replicate.roi` and became an override on the crop node's `region` parameter,
and a region is a mapping. Every copy in the chain copies the outer dict and
aliases the inner one.

What makes this worth a finding rather than a note is that the test suite says
otherwise and cannot notice. `test_an_override_read_out_cannot_be_written_back_in`
is the case written to pin exactly this claim, and it pins the scalar half —
`{"level": 0.5}` — while `make_project`, twenty lines away in the sibling file,
uses `{"region": {"x": 0, "y": 0, "width": 64, "height": 64}}` as its
representative override. The fixture that would have failed the case and the
case are in the same commit.

The prediction that would refute this: if the aliasing were confined to a read
path with no writer, a mutation through it would be a discipline problem rather
than a correctness one. It is not — `params_for` is the method the module
docstring names as the single definition of effective params, read by
`cache_key.py`, the executor, and a front end, and a front end holding a
parameter form is precisely a writer.
