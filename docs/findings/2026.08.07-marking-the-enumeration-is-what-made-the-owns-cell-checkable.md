# Marking the enumeration is what made the Owns cell checkable

The item asked for the three wrong lines and then for a verdict on whether the
agreement is checkable, with the warning that a weaker check installed quietly
is worse than none. The measurement above is what decides it: the degraded
check — packages against directories — is green on every one of the three
misses. It is not a weaker version of the question, it is a different question,
and installing it would have left a reader believing the lines were verified.

What made the strong version possible is that the "Owns" cell has two parts
that no punctuation reliably separates. `core` puts its qualifications after an
em-dash; `decode` puts *the summary* before one and the enumeration after; and
both carry inline ADR links. Any rule that splits the cell gets one of those
rows wrong. Marking the enumeration sidesteps the parse entirely, and it costs
the table nothing a reader notices except emphasis on the things the row is
about.

The price is real and is in the second measurement: only one line of eight
already spoke the table's words. Six were reworded not because they were wrong
but because containment is a literal test, so "the performance budgets" and
"the budget table" are two names for one thing and the gate cannot know it.
That is the trade — a checked copy of the decision's vocabulary in place of an
unchecked paraphrase of its meaning. It is the right side of the trade only
because the vocabulary is short: four phrases is the longest row.
