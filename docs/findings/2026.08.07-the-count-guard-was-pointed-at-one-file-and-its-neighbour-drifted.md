# The count guard was pointed at one file and its neighbour drifted

`tests/bench/test_budget_producers.py` exists because a prose count was wrong:
its docstring says so in as many words, naming an AUTO-GUARDRAILS section that
claimed 7-of-11 and 2-of-11 against a table of twelve with four and three. The
fix was to replace the counted claim with two sets a test compares against the
tree in both directions, and the fix worked — those sets are still exact at
`main`.

What it did not do is make counting safe anywhere else, and the file that
imports those sets is the one that got them wrong. `metrics.py` opens by saying
`budgets.py` declares eleven ceilings. It declared twelve by the time
`knob_to_first_partial` landed, and the paragraph three lines down inherited the
error — "four of the eleven" is the right four against the wrong total, which
is the tell that the numerator came from the set and the denominator from
memory.

The prediction that would have refuted this reading: if the twelfth row were
recent and `test_budget_producers.py` said eleven too, then the drift would be
one edit that missed two files rather than a claim nothing could check. It says
twelve. One file was checkable and stayed true; the neighbour was prose and did
not.

This is why v3's port restates the count rather than dropping it — a reader of
`metrics.py` genuinely wants to know how much of the table is dark — and why
the restatement is worth a guard rather than a habit.
