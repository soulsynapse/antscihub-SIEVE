# The loop budget is met headless, with two to three orders of margin

The tightest ratio in the table is the one that matters: `slider_to_preview` at
2.95 ms against 100 ms. Everything else has more room, and the ranking is not
what the anchors would predict — `open_to_first_frame`, the most generous
ceiling at 500 ms, has the *least* absolute room to grow because opening a
container costs ~10 ms regardless of how small the footage is, while every
per-frame number scales with a frame this clip has almost none of.

That is the caveat that outranks the margin. A 160x120 frame is 1/1100th of a
5.3K one. The pipeline's per-frame arithmetic — a crop, a block-grid difference,
a Morlet band power over a window — is close to linear in pixels, so a naive
extrapolation puts `slider_to_preview` past its ceiling on real footage by a
wide margin. That is not a defect and not a hidden failure: VISION's scope note
states these ceilings for the reference workload precisely so the promise is
conditioned on a workload rather than wished over all of them. What this
measurement establishes is that the *architecture* costs nothing — no session
setup, no plan rebuild, no store miss, no accidental re-decode is eating the
budget — and that is the thing a GUI can no longer prove once it is in the way.

The reuse number is where the docstring and the tree disagree, mildly.
`preview.py` argues that an edit below the roots decodes nothing and serves the
head from the store; that holds, and the cold-to-warm drop is it. But only the
`crop` node is actually keyed: `block_signal` and `detect` are stateful, so
`cache_policy` gives them no key and they recompute in full on every render. The
post-edit render therefore recomputes 58 of 87 node outputs and is still 4x
cheaper than the cold one, which means the saving being measured is the decode
and the docstring's "an edit below the roots decodes nothing" is exactly the
right claim while "serves the head from the store" is doing less work than it
sounds like. On a graph whose middle node were cacheable the two would separate,
and this clip cannot tell them apart.

What would have to change for the conclusion to flip: any single sample missing
its ceiling, which `test_no_single_sample_missed_its_ceiling` checks rather than
leaving to the median. The worst individual sample in the whole run is the cold
window render at 25.26 ms of 3000 ms.

That last sentence is true of the samples the test can read, and the review
found it does not read all of them:
`2026.08.07-the-per-sample-gate-cannot-see-the-cold-first-frame.md` measures
five `slider_to_preview` samples that the fixture clears before collecting, the
largest of them 15.59 ms — closer to its ceiling, in ratio, than anything in the
table above. The verdict stands; the margin is 6x rather than 34x at the one
point the gate cannot see.

## What changed when the admission rule did (2026-08-07, later the same day)

The closed question above is the one 06.5 acted on, and the re-run is in the
table: 58 recomputed became 30, and the store now saves the arithmetic as well
as the decode. One correction to the sentence that reported it — `detect` is
not stateful. It was refused a key for being `WINDOWED`, and `block_signal` for
being stateful, which is `adr/cache-admission-is-bounded-warmup.md`'s whole
point: one refusal was standing for two different properties and neither of
those two tools had the property it was actually for.

The residual 30 is not slack. Twenty-nine of them are the edited node, which has
a key nothing has ever written and must recompute by construction; the
thirtieth is `block_signal` at frame 0, where `decode_start` is clamped and its
one frame of warmup has nowhere to come from. A render of a window away from the
start of the footage has 29 of 87.

The medians moved with it and neither ceiling was close before or after, so the
verdict is unchanged. What the drop does say is that the caveat above — every
number is 160x120 — now has more room behind it than it did: the arithmetic
that scales with pixel count is the part the store stopped repeating.

## The corrected write guard costs none of it (2026-08-08)

06.5's re-take moved the guard from a node's own warmup to the accumulated one
([the write guard reads a node's own warmup](2026.08.07-the-write-guard-reads-a-nodes-own-warmup-and-the-lead-in-is-its-ancestors.md)),
which strictly narrows what may be keyed — so the question the fix raises is
whether the 30 above went back up. Same probe, same reference workload, this
machine: 87 of 87 on the cold render and 30 of 87 on each of the four
post-edit renders, which is the row above unchanged. Nothing was lost because
the frames the narrower guard withholds are lead-in frames, and the reuse
figure is counted over the span.

## The graph half of the gate, and it is the binding one (2026-08-08)

`slider_to_graph` was the open question this finding could not reach, and 06.6's
`pipeline/series_collector.py` gave it a subject: a refill is a post-edit window
render carried on to the stacked array a graph is drawn from, timed end to end.
Five edits on the warm store, median 5.12 ms against 200 ms.

39x is the narrowest ratio anywhere in the table, and the reason is structural
rather than a fact about this clip. Every other per-gesture ceiling is charged
for one frame; this one is charged for the whole working window, because a graph
is a picture of a span. So it scales with window length where `slider_to_preview`
does not, and it is the first in-pipeline number that will move when the window
grows — before the pixel count that the caveat above is about. The assembly is
not what costs: 0.22-0.26 ms of the span is the stack, and the rest is the render
inside it.

That also settles which of the two numbers Phase 7 should be watched against.
`slider_to_preview` at 54x is the one VISION's argument is stated on, and it has
the most room; `slider_to_graph` is the one a GUI can spend the margin of.

## The gate now reads the samples it could not (2026-08-08)

The caveat two sections up — "true of the samples the test can read" — no longer
applies. `Reading` keeps the run's samples whole beside the per-gate series the
fixture's clears narrow, and the gate is
`test_every_sample_the_run_published_is_gated` rather than
`test_no_single_sample_missed_its_ceiling`. The 15.59 ms cold first frame is
inside what it judges, so the 6x point is now guarded rather than merely
measured, and the worst-sample sentence above holds over the whole run instead
of over the survivors of a clear. No number moved; what moved is which of them a
red test would catch.
