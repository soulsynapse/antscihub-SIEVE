# The per-sample gate cannot see the largest sample of the ceiling it guards

`test_loop_budget.py`'s fixture clears the recorder between the window renders
and the drags, and its comment argues the case well: `render_window` publishes
`slider_to_preview` around its own first frame, which is a cold decode and not a
drag, and pooling it into the drag median would raise the ceiling that decides
whether direct manipulation is direct. All of that holds. The numbers say so —
15.59 ms pooled into a series whose members are ~2.9 ms would move the median by
roughly a third.

What the comment does not argue, because it was not deciding it, is that the
same line also removes those five samples from `Reading` entirely. So
`test_no_single_sample_missed_its_ceiling` — the test written precisely because
"a median that passes over a 400 ms sample is the preview a user calls janky" —
never sees the sample nearest its ceiling. The gated drags run at 1–3% of
`slider_to_preview`'s 100 ms; the dropped cold frame runs at 16%. Nothing is
missed today, and that is the point: the guard is 5x further from firing than
the numbers it is allowed to read suggest, and a regression would first appear
in the samples it cannot read.

For the conclusion to flip, the cold first frame would have to be cheaper than
the warm drags, or `render_window` would have to stop publishing under
`FIRST_FRAME_BUDGET`. It does publish — `preview.py` wraps the first frame in it
unconditionally, whether or not the whole window is being rendered — and the
cold sample is 27x the warm ones because it is the only one that decodes.
