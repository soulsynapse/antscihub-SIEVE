# The write guard reads a node's own warmup, and the lead-in belongs to its ancestors

`adr/cache-admission-is-bounded-warmup.md`'s gate is that a served range equals
a cold one exactly, and 06.5 built two halves of that: a re-settle for the state
the hits walk past, and a rule that the store is untouched until the node's
warmup has elapsed. The second half is the one that is short. It asks how far
back *this* node's output depends and never asks the same question of the frames
being fed to it, so the guard is exactly right at a root and progressively wrong
underneath one.

The reason it looks right is that until 06.5 no keyed tool had a warmup at all.
Every tool the old policy admitted — `crop`, `downsample`, `normalize`,
`rescale`, `span` — declares `warmup_frames = 0`, so `first + warmup` was
`first` for every keyed node and there was no lead-in window to be wrong about.
Admitting `block_signal` puts a warmed tool at the top of a keyed chain for the
first time, and the guard's blind spot becomes reachable in the same commit that
writes the guard.

The negative result worth stating: if the guard were reading the accumulated
warmup, `norm`'s first entry in run one would be 60 rather than 59 and run two
would have no mismatch. That is the check, and it is one line of arithmetic
away — which is why the failure is a boundary and not a design problem with the
admission rule.

`detect` in the oracle graph is the case that shows why the criterion did not
catch this. It also writes one lead-in entry, and that entry happens to be
correct: `node_warmup_frames` returns 22 for the configuration while the tool's
arithmetic reaches only `window_frames = 9` back, so the under-warmed frame at
the far edge of its handed span never enters the answer. An entry being right
by a margin nobody declared is not the same as a guard being right, and the
margin is the subject of
[detect's two window bounds are the same number](2026.08.07-detects-two-window-bounds-are-the-same-number-and-the-case-cannot-see-it.md).

## Amendment, 2026-08-08: the guard reads the per-node fold, and one named test was innocent

The open question is answered by the exact side. `BoundNode` gains
`upstream_warmup`, the maximum of its parents' accumulated lead-in, and
`bound.lead_in` is that plus its own warmup — the forward mirror of the
`upstream_lag`/`lag` pair already in `_bind`, folded the same way and in the same
index space, since `rate_changing` is refused there. `plan.lead_in` would have
been the conservative alternative and it is exactly this quantity maximised over
the roots; taking the maximum costs reuse at every node that is not the one the
fold came from, and the exact fold is one `max()` beside a `max()` that was
already being written.

The second consequence above is half wrong and the half matters.
`test_cache_admission.test_the_store_is_never_written_before_a_nodes_own_warmup_has_elapsed`
did pin the boundary an ancestor's warmup too early, and is corrected and renamed.
`test_executor.test_the_store_holds_no_frame_computed_before_its_nodes_warmup_elapsed`
did not: its graph is the single node `t`, which is a root, and at a root the
accumulated warmup *is* the node's own. It was green before the fix and after it,
unedited. What the two have in common is the expression `first + own warmup`;
what separates them is whether anything sits above the node, which is the whole
subject of this finding and is the reason reading the expression was not enough.

Both directions of the guard are now killed by
`tests/unit/test_cache_admission.py` under
`scripts/mutation_sweep.py` — `bound.lead_in ==> bound.warmup` at the guard, and
`upstream_warmup + warmup ==> warmup` at the property.
