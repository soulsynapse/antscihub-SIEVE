# Removing the arrow case makes the combo's signal choice unpinnable rather than pinned

The item this came from recorded the swap as an open question and predicted its
own fix would close it: "the choice of signal is unpinned until a case
exercises a closed combo under arrow keys." That reads as an ordering — write
the case, and the mutant dies. It is the opposite. The reason `activated` was
the wrong signal on a closed combo is that Qt calls arrowing a closed combo an
act of selection; the fix stops a closed combo from having any arrow behaviour
at all, so after it there is no state in which the two signals disagree. A
prediction that a fix will make a mutant die is worth writing down precisely
because the fix can instead make it equivalent, and the two look the same from
the survivor table.

What the sweep does buy is the other four rules, all of which die. The one that
nearly did not is the combo's `wheelEvent`: the first draft of the test flicked
downward over an enum whose default is its last choice, so Qt had nowhere to
step and the override could be deleted without the test noticing. That is the
general shape of a wheel case over a bounded control — a notch toward the end
the control is already at is indistinguishable from a notch that was declined —
and it is why the test now flicks both ways.
