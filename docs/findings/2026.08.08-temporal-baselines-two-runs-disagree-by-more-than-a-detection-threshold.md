# temporal_baseline's two runs disagree by more than a detection threshold

The tool's module docstring already argued that two runs meeting at one frame
hold different samples of the same span, "which is a tolerance and not zero".
That was right about the sign and wrong about the size. The tolerance is not
small compared to the thing it is denominated in: a user thresholds this tool's
output at 3-5 deviations, and the p99 disagreement between two runs is 1.5 while
the maximum is 63-93. Where the run started can decide whether a cell fires.

**The prediction that failed.** If the sampling argument were the whole story,
the divergence would scale with the median's standard error — a few percent of
the estimate, falling as `1/sqrt(n)` and roughly flat across window lengths
since the ring holds `MAX_SAMPLES` either way. The baseline emission does
exactly that. The deviation emission does not, and the ratio between the two
(87-126x against the frame's own scale, versus 0.35-0.46x) is the size of the
effect the sampling argument does not contain.

The mechanism is the denominator. A deviation is `(x - median) / (1.4826 * MAD)`
and both statistics are estimated from the run's own sample of the span. Where
the spread estimate is small the ratio is large, and where it is *zero*
`_floored` substitutes the frame's median positive spread — a substitution that
happens per run, so one run can divide a cell by its own near-zero MAD while the
other divides the same cell by a frame-wide value orders of magnitude larger.
Nothing bounds that ratio, which is why the declared number is a measured
ceiling rather than a bound and why the finding says so in `open_questions`
rather than in a comment that would read as a guarantee.

The stride-1 row is the control: a window under `MAX_SAMPLES` frames makes both
runs admit every frame, the windows become identical, and the difference is
exactly zero. So the divergence is the sample *phase* and not the window's
finiteness — the module docstring's claim, confirmed, with a number attached.
