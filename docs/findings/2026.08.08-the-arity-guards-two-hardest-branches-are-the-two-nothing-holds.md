# The arity guard's two hardest branches are the two nothing holds

The shape is not that the guard is wrong. It refuses `span`'s old two-int
declaration, it accepts `detect`'s bare pairs and `crop`'s `ROI`, and both
directions are asserted. It is that the two branches carrying the most
reasoning — the ones whose behaviour a reader could not derive and so are
written out in the docstring — are exactly the two no fixture can disagree
with, because both were written for tools that do not exist yet.

That is the guard's whole purpose. `_ONE_FIELD_STEREOTYPES` is spelled as the
exception so a kind minted later and forgotten is refused rather than waved
through; the union and variadic branches are the same bet on a tool nobody has
written. A bet the suite does not hold is a comment, not a check, and the next
worker to touch the function will be told by nothing that `min` was the load-
bearing word.

What would refute the framing: if a registered tool declared a union with two
non-`None` branches, or any variadic annotation, the branches would be covered
by the shelf and this would be a naming complaint about the test comment alone.
`detect.count_frac` is the closest and is `tuple[float, float] | None` — the
one-branch case.
