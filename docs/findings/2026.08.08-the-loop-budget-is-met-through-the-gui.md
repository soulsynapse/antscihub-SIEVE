# The loop budget is met through the GUI

Phase 6 was run before Phase 7 for one reason, stated in `test_loop_budget.py`'s
own docstring: a number taken through the GUI can no longer tell a slow pipeline
from a slow paint. This is the reading that cashes it, and the answer is
readable precisely because both numbers exist.

The in-pipeline regime is unchanged by the window. `slider_to_graph` — the key
VISION's product claim is stated on, drag a slider and the graphs refill — is
4.86 ms through five spin box edits against 5.12 ms measured headless over the
same five window values. That is not "close"; it is inside what two passes of
the same headless probe disagree by
([the loop budget is met headless](2026.08.07-the-loop-budget-is-met-headless.md),
last section). Whatever the window adds — a `QTimer` turn, a `mark_stale`, a
`set_series`, and Qt's own repaint scheduling — is under the resolution of a 5 ms
span. The refill is the render, and the render did not move.

The pre-pipeline regime is where the window costs something, and it is worth
being precise about what. Headless, `scrub_to_repaint` was `VideoReader.read` and
nothing else: 0.25 ms. Through the window it is a queued signal onto the decode
thread, the same read, a resample to `PROXY_WIDTH`, a `QImage` construction, a
queued signal back, and the canvas' synchronous update — 2.87 ms. The markup is
11x and the ratio to the ceiling is still 35x, so it changes no decision. What it
does is locate the window's cost: essentially all of it is in the transport, and
essentially none in the tuning loop.

That split is also the reason the markup is a floor rather than a fraction. Two
queued signal deliveries and a thread hop cost what they cost regardless of frame
size, while the decode and the resample scale with it. On 5.3K footage v2
measured ~68 ms for the seek alone, so the window's 2.6 ms would be noise there —
which is the opposite of how the per-frame numbers extrapolate, and the reason
this row is worth keeping separately from the headless one rather than folding
them.

## What `slider_to_preview` measures here, and why it is not the headless number

*Superseded by the amendment below, which is where the current reading is. This
section is the 07.11 record and is left as it was written.*

0.32 ms against 1.95 ms headless, and the difference is not an improvement. The
two readings are of two different things:

Headless, the key was published by `render_frame` — eight single-frame renders at
eight playhead positions, each running the whole graph for one frame. Through the
window, nothing calls `render_frame` at all. `gui/tuning.py` renders spans,
because a graph is drawn from a span; the frame under the playhead comes from the
transport, and the canvas is showing decoded *source* footage rather than
pipeline output. So the samples here are the first frame of each window render,
and on a warm store the span's first frame is very nearly free.

The key is gated anyway, because its producer is one this cut drives and a
ceiling nothing judges is not a ceiling. But its label says "preview repaint",
and there is no repaint of a rendered frame in this cut to judge —
`todo/the-viewport-shows-the-source-and-not-the-render.md` is where that is
written down, and until it lands, this row is a measurement of a render nobody
sees rather than of the interval the ceiling names.

What would have to be true for that to be a problem today: the number would have
to be *used* to argue the loop feels direct. It is not; `slider_to_graph` is,
and that one is measured on exactly what it claims.

## Amendment, 2026-08-08: the viewport paints the render, and the key means what it says

`todo/the-viewport-shows-the-source-and-not-the-render.md` landed. The canvas is
now fed the watched node's output for the frame under the playhead, through
`preview.render_frame` off the session's own store, and `slider_to_preview` is
gated on the playhead moves that produce it rather than on the first frame of a
window render. Same harness, same clip, same machine and build as the rows above;
the medians were read off `Reading.median_ms` the same way those were.

| key | 07.11 | now | ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| `slider_to_preview` | 0.32 ms (5 window first frames) | **1.91 ms** (8 playhead moves) | 100 ms |
| `scrub_to_repaint` | 2.87 ms | **5.38 ms** | 100 ms |
| `slider_to_graph` | 4.86 ms | 4.84 ms | 200 ms |
| `full_preview_render` | 4.50 ms | 4.57 ms | 3000 ms |

The first row is the point. 1.91 ms against the headless 1.95 ms — the two
readings are now of the same gesture, and they agree to within what two passes of
the headless probe disagree by. The 0.32 ms above was not a faster window; it was
a cheaper question, and the gap between the two numbers is exactly the size of
the thing that was not being measured.

The second row is what it cost. A playhead move now runs a single-frame render
inside the transport's round trip — the render happens in the `frame_changed`
slot, and `player.py` publishes `scrub_to_repaint` after that emit on purpose, so
the picture the user is scrubbing to is inside the number. 2.87 → 5.38 ms is that
render, and it is 19x inside the ceiling rather than 35x. This is a real charge
against the pre-pipeline regime and worth watching on large footage, where the
render scales with pixels and the 2.6 ms of thread-hop overhead does not.

Neither of the two in-pipeline rows moved, which is the check that the second
render did not disturb the first: the graph's refill still serves everything
above the edited node out of the store, and the viewport's frame is one more
reader of it.

Worst sample anywhere in the pass is still the session's cold first frame, now
15.32 ms under `slider_to_preview` — 6x inside its ceiling, and unchanged in
character.

## Amendment, 2026-08-09: the drag gives the render back, and the settle keeps it

`todo/the-scrub-round-trip-now-carries-a-pipeline-render.md` was ruled: the
render is skipped while a drag is in flight and painted on the settle, which
`RequestKind.SCRUB` is what expresses. Same harness, clip, machine and build as
the rows above; three consecutive passes rather than one, because the point of
this reading is a difference of 2.4 ms and two of the four keys move by that
much between passes anyway.

| key | 07.11 | 07.12 | now (3 passes) | ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `scrub_to_repaint` | 2.87 ms | 5.38 ms | **2.99 / 2.98 / 2.98 ms** | 100 ms |
| `slider_to_preview` | 0.32 ms | 1.91 ms | **2.11 / 1.95 / 1.87 ms** | 100 ms |
| `slider_to_graph` | 4.86 ms | 4.84 ms | 5.78 / 5.20 / 4.95 ms | 200 ms |
| `full_preview_render` | 4.50 ms | 4.57 ms | 5.36 / 4.85 / 4.65 ms | 3000 ms |

The first row is the charge being handed back: 5.38 → 2.98 ms, which is the
07.11 number to within the spread of a single key across three passes. A drag
is again the decode leg and the thread hop and nothing else, so the number
`ScrubPolicy` degrades on is once more a number coarse mode can do something
about — which is the whole of what the ruling bought, and it is a property of
the pairing rather than of this clip.

The second row is what was *not* given back, and is the check that the ruling
was carried out rather than the render simply deleted: `slider_to_preview` is
gated on the playhead moves, which are `seek` and therefore settles, and it did
not move. The exact picture is still rendered and still measured; only the
in-flight drag stopped paying for one.

The two in-pipeline rows moved by more than the change could explain and by
less than the spread between these three passes, taken on a machine with an
agent running on it — which is the reason to read them as a band rather than as
three medians. Nothing here touches the tuning loop.

## What would flip the conclusion

Any single sample missing its ceiling, which
`test_every_sample_the_session_published_is_gated` checks over the whole pass
rather than over the survivors of a clear — including the cold render, whose
16.42 ms first frame is the worst sample anywhere in this run and the closest
anything came, at 6x, to a limit.
