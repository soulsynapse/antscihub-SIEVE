# A pixel band beside a lane reaches the next lane's arrowhead

The occlusion claim in `tests/gui/test_chain_edges.py` reads three columns at a
chosen row and is safe for it: what it looks for is one line, and it looks at
where the line is. A *name* has no such point — glyph coverage at this size is
sparse and which columns are inked is the font's — so the honest read is a band,
and a band is what makes the assertion reachable by things that are not the name.

What would have made this a true positive is a stack one lane wide. The failure
needs two writes into the output card, which is the only fan-in the picture has,
and that is exactly the case the label exists for: the mockup names a port only
where a step has more than one input. So the case that gives the name a reason to
exist is the case that satisfies its pixel assertion for free, and the two arrived
in the same test.

The bound that holds is geometric rather than typographic: EDGE_LANE is 34, an
arrowhead's shoulder is 4 either side, and the name's origin is 12 right of the
lane — so anything past 12 + 18 belongs to the next edge. Sizing the window to
the text instead would grow it every time a product name got longer.
