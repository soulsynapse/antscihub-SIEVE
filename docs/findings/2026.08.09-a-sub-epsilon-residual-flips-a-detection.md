# A residual under the declared epsilon flips a detection

The refutation this was designed to admit would have looked like a count that
did not move: two runs whose foregrounds differ in the fourth decimal, a Morlet
band power that averages the difference away over its window, and an in-band
count that lands on the same integer for both because a block either is in the
value band or is not. That is a real mechanism and it is why the ADR left the
question open rather than answering it from the armchair — `inband_count` is a
quantizer, and quantizers are where small differences go to die.

It does not happen, and the reason is that the count is *windowed* before it is
thresholded. A single block crossing the value floor at one frame out of fifteen
moves the windowed mean by 1/15 of a block, which is a real number rather than
an integer, and the gate compares against it. So the quantizer that should have
absorbed the residual is followed immediately by an average that un-quantizes it.
Over 80 frames, 0.20 blocks of movement — three frames' worth of one block
crossing — is what survives.

The 3% figure is the one that decides the question rather than the 0.28%. A
residual is only dangerous where a threshold might be, and a session's threshold
is not placed uniformly at random: it is dragged down until the detection appears
and left there, which puts it against the shoulder of the count's range, which is
where the two runs differ. The tuning gesture and the failure mode share an
address.

For the conclusion to flip, the count would have to move by less than the
smallest band a user can place a handle in — which is a claim about the GUI's
slider resolution and not about the arithmetic, and it would have to hold on
footage with far more contrast than this fixture has. Neither is worth pursuing:
`adr/correctness-is-the-default.md` already sets the bar at bit-identity, and
this measurement is what says that bar is not merely conservative.

## Amendment 2026-08-09: the uint8 chain does not rescue it

The first worry about carrying this in float32 was that VISION's lead scenario is
a crop feeding `background_ema` in uint8, where `_narrow`'s rounding would send a
residual of 0.298 to zero on most pixels. Measured, the uint8 run's residual is a
full level and its count difference is *larger* — 0.4667 blocks against a 6.4083
range, 7.3%. Rounding does not average the residual away; it rounds a fraction of
a level up to a whole one wherever the two models straddle a boundary, which is
more pixels than it silences. The committed case runs in float32 for a reason
that has nothing to do with the answer: the uint8 premise assertion would sit
within 7% of its own bound and go red on any edit to the fixture.
