# An orphaned pane takes activation from the window offscreen

The neighbouring finding
(`2026.08.09-a-key-sent-to-a-widget-inside-the-window-is-taken-by-the-walks-hotkey.md`)
established that a window shortcut is matched on the way *to* a child, which is
why these cases send the key to the pane rather than to the window. This is the
other half of the same precondition and it was invisible until a sixth case
landed: the shortcut is also matched only while the window is the *active* one,
and the fixture's `activateWindow()` is not the last word on that. Whatever the
previous case left orphaned can take it.

What would refute the reading: if the failure tracked how many windows the
session had opened, the cause would be the fixture's activation being lost to
accumulation rather than to a specific widget. It does not — one pinning case
ahead of the P case is enough, and two non-pinning cases ahead of it are not.
A pin is what puts a rebuilt pane in the bin, which is what makes the orphan.

The measured cost of the direct fix is why the test is ordered instead: draining
the queue a second time inside the fixture delivers a transport failure the
cases above were written against, so making one case robust breaks another. The
fixture cannot be made order-independent without deciding what it means for a
window over footage that does not exist to have settled, which is a larger
question than this item.
