# The epsilon chain repeats its lead-in arithmetic on every render, not its decode

The interesting half is the half that was wrong. `preview.py` argues at length
that an epsilon-warmup node makes every render pay its lead-in, and the sentence
it says it in names the decode first: such a graph "decodes and runs its whole
lead-in on every single render — 90 frames for `background_ema`". The decode is
the expensive half in every other paragraph of that module, and it is the half
that does not happen here.

What actually happens is that the crop above the model keeps its key, and a key
covers the lead-in frames as well as the span's — `cache.py` keys by
`(node key, source frame index)` and a lead-in frame has an index like any other.
So the cold render writes 40 crop entries for a 10-frame window, and every render
after it reads all 40 back and calls the three unkeyed tools below on top of them.
The reader is asked for nothing. The claim survives with its subject changed:
what repeats is the arithmetic, and the arithmetic is what a background model
mostly is.

That changes what the remedy has to be. If the cost were the decode, a wider
prefetch or a proxy would touch it; because it is the tools, only a materialized
product upstream of the model removes it, which is the checkpoint the todo
already names. And it narrows where the docstring's version is true: an epsilon
node with nothing keyed above it — a `background_ema` reading the footage
directly, with no crop — really does re-decode, because there is no stored
upstream for the loop to walk instead. VISION's lead scenario has a crop in it,
so the ordinary case is the cheaper one.

The number that decides scheduling is 75 against 10. Both chains answer for the
same 10 frames of the same footage after the same edit at the same leaf; the
spliced one calls tools seven and a half times as often, and 65 of those 75 calls
are for frames the user is not looking at. The unspliced chain's post-edit render
was already measured at 6.6 ms
(findings/2026.08.07-the-loop-budget-is-met-headless.md), against a 3 s ceiling,
so 7.5x is not by itself a missed budget on this clip — and this clip is
160x120. The reading is here so that the ratio is re-runnable when the resolution
is not.
