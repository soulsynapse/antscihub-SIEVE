# The offering derivation is free at redraw scale

The worry this answers is structural rather than numerical: a question that was
asked once, of one open box, became a question asked of every card on every move
of the walk — and the interactive tuning loop is the constraint the architecture
serves, so a derivation moving from once-per-gesture to N-per-redraw is worth a
number before it is worth a shrug.

Had the mechanism been expensive it would have shown as a shelf construction
cost: `_shelf()` calls `ToolRegistry.latest` per id and is rebuilt inside every
`_offer_at`, so the sweep pays fourteen shelf-sized lookups plus fourteen ×
fourteen `matches` comparisons. That is 196 spec comparisons and it does not
register. `matches` is a set comparison over two small frozen fields
(`core/tool_base.StreamSpec`), which is why: there is no parse, no allocation
of anything document-sized, and nothing touches disk.

The number to watch is the shelf's size, because the sweep is quadratic in it —
fourteen tools today. A shelf of two hundred would be 40,000 comparisons and
worth measuring again; nothing about this result says the quadratic is safe, only
that it is far from binding at the size the shelf is.
