# The shelf declares too little for eight of ten positions to offer anything

The prediction, had the ruling been wrong, was that `matches` would behave like
`admits` with a smaller constant — a shortlist of six or seven instead of ten,
useless for the same reason. It does not: the distribution is bimodal, 2 or 0,
and which of the two a position gets is decided entirely by whether its
upstream's `emits` states `channels`. That is the predicate discriminating on
exactly what it was designed to discriminate on, and it is why the eight zeros
are a fact about the shelf rather than about the comparison.

The number to watch is the count of tools whose `emits` leaves a field empty.
Eight of ten today; every one of them is a preserving or dtype-agnostic tool,
so it will not fall by tools being more careful — it falls when something
resolves the stream fields along the walk, or not at all.

Consequence for the box: it opens at every gap and renders an empty offer with a
sentence where it has nothing, because most gaps are that case and a gesture
that refused there would leave the user unable to reach the gaps that offer.

## Amendment 2026-08-09: the binding constraint is `accepts`, and two tools can ever be offered

Measured while taking
`todo/the-stream-a-position-produces-is-resolved-not-declared`, whose plan is to
resolve the produced stream along the walk and hand *that* to `offered_tools` in
place of the upstream's declared `emits`. This probe is over the required side
rather than the produced one, because `ArraySpec._unused` returns `None` — no
match — when the *required* tuple is empty exactly as it does when the produced
one is, and nothing had asked which tools that leg alone eliminates.

| tool | `accepts.dtypes` | `accepts.channels` | offerable anywhere |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `background_ema` | 4 dtypes | — | no |
| `block_signal` | 4 dtypes | — | no |
| `checkpoint` | — | — | no |
| `crop` | — | — | no |
| `detect` | `float32` | `gray` | yes |
| `downsample` | 4 dtypes | — | no |
| `farneback` | `uint8` | — | no |
| `footage` | — | — | no |
| `motion_history` | `float32` `float64` | `gray` | yes |
| `normalize` | 4 dtypes | — | no |
| `pick` | — | — | no |
| `rescale` | 4 dtypes | — | no |
| `span` | — | — | no |
| `temporal_baseline` | `float32` `float64` | — | no |

Twelve of fourteen leave an `accepts` field unstated, so twelve of fourteen match
nothing — for every produced spec that exists, and for every one a resolution
could ever hand the predicate. The two that can be offered both require
`float32` and `gray`, and every source on the shelf emits `uint8`, so the offer
at a source position and at every position below it up to the first conversion
to float32 is empty however completely the produced side is resolved.

The number this file said to watch — the count of tools whose `emits` leaves a
field empty — is therefore not the number that moves the offer. Resolving `emits`
forward converges the produced side and leaves the required side as it was, which
is why the "offered" column reads `detect motion_history` or nothing in every row
of both probes above: those two are the whole of what the shelf can offer, and
the eight and then ten zeros are counting the positions that fail to reach them.

Consequence for the item: a non-empty offer needs a ruling on what an unstated
`accepts` field means at an offer — the leg `ArraySpec.matches` documents as
"ignorance is legal and never plausible", stated of both sides — which that item
puts out of scope in the same paragraph that names a long offer as its target.
Both cannot hold, and which gives is a decision rather than a measurement.
