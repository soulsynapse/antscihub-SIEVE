# The display channel costs a watched node its re-use, and the band budget holds

The review that widened `todo/a-declared-surface-is-drawn-by-nothing.md` asked
for this measurement *before* the picture was built, on the grounds that a
picture assembled first would make a change to the channel look like a change to
the drawing code. The prediction it was worth testing was specific: `display`
runs `_chain` a second time on top of `run`'s and then `morlet_power_profile`
over the *whole* bank where `run` sums only the band's rows, and returns one
column of each while discarding the rest of a window that is
`warmup + lookahead + 1` frames wide — 59 frames of transform per frame of
output at `golden_params`. A watched node is also never served from the store, so
none of it amortizes across a re-drag.

Every part of that is true and the ceiling holds regardless. The warm re-render
goes from 2–5 ms to a flat 26 ms, which is the two costs together: the node
stops being served (the store hit count drops by exactly the detector's rows) and
the second derivation runs. What the flatness says is that the fill dominates —
a served-versus-computed difference would still vary with what the edit
invalidated, and this does not.

What would have flipped the conclusion is a warm figure above about 40 ms, which
would have left no room for the window's own contribution. The window's
contribution turns out to be small: 28.5 ms measured through a real drag against
26 ms measured headless, so the widget layer costs a couple of milliseconds and
the render is the whole of it.

The number that should be carried forward is the multiplier rather than the
milliseconds. On a 29-frame span of 320×240 footage nothing is at its real size,
and a ceiling met by 21 ms here is not a ceiling met by 21 ms on 5.3K. The claim
that survives a change of footage is that filling the three surfaces costs about
six times a warm re-render of the same window, and that the redundancy inside
`display` is where that six comes from.
