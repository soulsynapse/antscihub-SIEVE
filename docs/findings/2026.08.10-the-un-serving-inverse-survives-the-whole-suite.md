# The un-serving inverse survives the whole suite

The footage field's removal forced a repair in `pipeline/crop_serving.py`:
`crop_roots` used to recognise a crop of the footage as a *root* crop node, and
once the footage is a source node above it, no crop is a root any more. The
forward half of the repair is well covered — narrowing `crop_roots` back to
roots-only fails eleven cases across the serving and binding suites, which is
what a mechanism that would otherwise have silently retired should look like.

The reverse half is not. `_uncut` now recomputes the graph's footage root and
adds an edge from it to every crop node it restores, and its docstring says why
in the strongest terms available: "The edge `_wired` cut is put back, which is
what makes the pair an edit and its undo rather than two edits: a crop of the
footage reads the footage root, and a restored crop node left as a root would be
a crop of the run's reader in a document that no longer has one." Delete that
edge restoration and the entire suite is green.

The two cases that would be expected to see it —
`test_a_served_project_can_be_cut_again` and
`test_a_replicate_added_to_a_served_project_runs`, which are the `done_when` of
[a served project has no way back to its crop node](../todo/a-served-project-has-no-way-back-to-its-crop-node.md)
— do notice a *reordered* footage root (reversing `_first_decoded_root`'s walk
fails both), so they reach `_uncut`'s parent lookup and assert something about
its answer. What they do not assert is that the answer is used. That is the
distinction worth keeping: a case that exercises a helper is not a case that
pins the helper's effect, and a round trip is the shape where the two are
easiest to confuse, because the state under test is reachable from either end.

Not a defect claim. The shipped code is right by its own argument; what is
missing is anything that would go red if a later edit dropped it.
