The v1/v2-shaped analysis ops, priced per regime (ms/frame, p50; decode for
scale from the 01/05 results):

| op               | small 462² | cut 1024² | uncut 5.3K |
|------------------|-----------:|----------:|-----------:|
| (decode)         | 0.04       | 0.10      | 5.3        |
| absdiff          | 0.013      | 0.34      | 5.5        |
| bgsub + count    | 0.039      | 0.69      | 8.6        |
| Farneback flow   | 33.9       | 177.7     | 2593       |
| Farneback ½-res  | 9.4        | 47.6      | 709        |
| DIS ultrafast    | 1.5        | 4.4       | 51.8       |
| DIS medium       | 8.8        | 26.5      | 501        |

Dense flow is 1000x decode post-cut and the only op in its cost class; frame
differencing, background subtraction and reductions are noise. The flow
algorithm choice is therefore worth more than every decode optimisation
combined: Farneback caps the small-clip pipeline at 29 fps (half realtime),
DIS-ultrafast runs it at ~660 fps. Whether DIS's field is good enough for
the events is unvalidated — that is a behaviour question, not a benchmark
one. Even DIS wants the compute-once-cache side of the pipeline (~46 s per
whole small video), with tuning living downstream of the cached field.

The Morlet CWT never dominates: ~20 ms per whole 64-scale transform of an
11k-sample series, ~47 ms for 30k — *if* the FFT length is padded to a
power of two. Raw, the 30,579-sample series (3 × prime) takes numpy down
its Bluestein path and costs 305 ms, 6.5x, for nothing. Pad-to-fast-length
is implementation law for the spectral layer.

## How to recreate

```
uv run --group experiments python experiments/decode-experiments/06-analysis-costs.py
```

Needs `video-tests/derived/cut-crf18-intra.mp4` (run 05-the-cut.py first if
absent). The CWT cases are synthetic series at the two files' frame counts;
image ops run on pre-decoded real luma so the costs are pure compute.
