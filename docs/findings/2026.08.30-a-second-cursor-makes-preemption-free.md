`2026.08.30-the-pressure-dispatcher-preempts-into-seeks` established that a
preemption is a seek out and a seek back, and closed by naming two rules it
did not implement: refuse to leave a sequential run for a position that run
will reach anyway, or do not pay to rejoin it. Both are now implemented as
flags on the explorer, defaulting off, along with a third the trace implied.
The second rule wins, it is free, and it removes the live/parked gap
entirely.

Window-covered wall per leg, `--walk --live-playhead`, all arms against the
same script:

| leg | parked control (n=2) | live control (n=6) | `--gui-cursor` (n=4) | `--keep-passed` (n=2) | `--abandon-within 480` (n=2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2, A cold | 7.3–7.4 | 7.4–33.9 | 6.4–7.0 | 6.9–7.1 | 4.6–4.8 |
| 4, B seam | 7.0–7.1 | 7.8–33.3 | 6.4–6.9 | 22.7–24.0 | 4.8–5.2 |
| 5, A again | 7.0–7.2 | 11.4–12.5 | 6.1–6.6 | 11.2–12.6 | 5.3–5.6 |

**A live playhead under `--gui-cursor` costs what a parked one costs.** Every
one of the twelve live `--gui-cursor` legs lands at or below the parked
control's floor. The gap the earlier finding measured between its parked and
live conditions — 6.6/6.6/6.3 against 35.1/22.4/11.2 — was not the price of
serving an interactive consumer. It was one cursor being shared.

**Preemption is not what costs; the rejoin is.** Under `--gui-cursor` the
alternations still happen, at the same rate or higher, and stop mattering.
Leg 4 runs at 50–62 role alternations against control runs at 46–52 that took
24 s, and `fill:seek` is 1 rather than 24–27. Leg 5 is the sharper case
because the control there is tight: 108–132 alternations against the
control's 18–22 — five times the preemption — and the wall halves, 12.4 s to
6.3 s. A dispatcher that is interrupted six times as often while the fill
reads sequentially throughout is the arrangement the earlier finding could
not distinguish from a dispatcher that is not interrupted at all.

**`fill:seek` is 1 per 480 in eleven of twelve legs**, which is what "the
sweep is a near-perfect sequential reader" looks like when nothing takes its
cursor away. The remaining seek is the one that lands the window.

**The second decoder is free.** Parked, where nothing declares INTERACTIVE
and the second container opens and sits, `--gui-cursor` is 6.6–7.0 against
the control's 7.0–7.4 — marginally faster, because it also removes the
residual `fill:seek`. The contention `decode-experiments/07-contention` and
`tool-experiments/04-under-load` measured does not appear, because the second
reader decodes only when there is an interactive pick to serve and is idle
otherwise. This answers `orchestrator-experiments` README question 5 in its
rewritten form: the graph's knowledge of what every consumer declared does
assign a second reader better than the fixed assignment, and urgency is the
axis it assigns on.

**The refusal rule works and is strictly worse.** `--abandon-within 480`
drives alternations to 0 and takes the wall to 4.6–5.6, the fastest arm — by
declining to serve the person. `gui:seek` goes to 0 and the GUI's `near`
stand-in falls from 83 serves per run to 2.5. It buys the wall with the thing
the wall exists for. At 120 it is a partial version of the same trade. The
rule is sound and it is the wrong one to reach for while a second cursor is
available, because that one costs nothing.

**Keeping the frames a step passes over does nothing.** `--keep-passed`
deposits every position the fetcher decodes on its way to a target instead of
discarding it, on the argument that a step of 40 throws away 39 positions the
sweep had declared and then seeks back for them. Leg 4 sits at 22.7–24.0 s
with `fill:seek` 28/28 at 54–58 alternations — indistinguishable from the
control at the same alternation count. The frames are saved and the rejoin is
a seek regardless, so it never touches the term that carries the wall.

## The cost model held; the seek count is a race

The earlier finding's model survives. Fitted here on leg 4's control runs,
`wall ≈ 5.1 s + 0.35 s × alternations`, and at two alternations per
interruption that is 0.70 s against its 0.62 s. Median `seek_ms` is 287–400
across every run, every leg and every arm, and `step_ms` 6.1–9.9; per-decode
cost is not what varies.

What that finding got wrong is the sentence "nothing here is noise: the six
walls span 6.3 to 35.1 s because the six legs apply six different interaction
loads." The walk's interaction load is fixed — `random.Random(11)`, fixed
sleeps, the same positions every run — and leg 4's alternation count ranges 8
to 82 over six runs of it, carrying the wall from 7.8 s to 33.3 s. How often
the GUI wins the queue while the fill is mid-run is a race between the Qt
timer and the dispatcher loop, not a property of the script. Its leg-for-leg
reproduction drew the high branch twice. The explorer's own `_land_at`
already recorded the same swing, 7 s to 33 s, against the play timer.

So a policy arm read against a single control run is unreadable, and
`fill:seek` out of `dispatch_trace` is the better instrument than the wall
wherever both are available: it is 1 or 2 under `--gui-cursor` in every
single leg, and 5–42 in the control, with no overlap.

## What this did not establish

**What the GUI's own service does.** Exact-hit rate rises under
`--gui-cursor` (34% to 37% of requests) and the `near` stand-in falls (23% to
13%), but the arms cannot be compared on this axis with this instrument: a
faster leg is a shorter leg, the play timer fires fewer times over a
different part of the fill, and the request denominators are not the same
experiment. The wall is measured here; felt latency is not.

**One topology, one file, one window size, one machine.** Three nodes — GUI,
sweep, tool — over one 480-frame window at full source form, single
dispatcher. Whether a second cursor still pays when there are four
interactive consumers, or when the second reader is decoding continuously
rather than in bursts, is untouched.

**Nothing about the production tree.** These are experiment modules. `serve`,
`fill` and `proxy` in `src/` still hold the fixed reader assignment this
result argues against.

## How to recreate

```
uv run --group experiments python experiments/orchestrator-experiments/explorer.py --walk --live-playhead
uv run --group experiments python experiments/orchestrator-experiments/explorer.py --walk --live-playhead --gui-cursor
```

Both arms need n greater than one; the control especially. `topology.preemption`
in the log names the arm, and `dispatch_trace` is the ordered
`(t, role, position, how, ms)` the alternation and `fill:seek` counts above are
sliced from, per leg, by each leg's `window covered` stamp.
