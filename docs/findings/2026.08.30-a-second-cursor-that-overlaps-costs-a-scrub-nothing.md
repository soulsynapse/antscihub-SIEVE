`2026.08.30-a-second-cursor-makes-preemption-free` showed that a preemption's
cost is one cursor being taken from the sweep rather than the price of serving
a person, and `2026.08.30-the-remaining-wall-is-decode-and-a-reader-that-does-
not-overlap` found the ceiling that stopped at: the wall is `fill + gui` and
not `max(fill, gui)`, because V1 has one dispatcher thread and `exact()` blocks
it whichever container it holds. That finding called the overlap a lead and not
a result, because nothing there ran two readers concurrently.

V2 does. Each fetch thread owns its own `Fetcher`, and above one reader the
bands **partition** — reader 0 never takes an INTERACTIVE pick, so the
sequential cursor is never moved by a person, and claims stop two readers
decoding one row.

A 160-frame window filling attention-first, against a scripted hand scrubbing
a gaussian random walk every 50 ms — the same shape V1's `_walk_scrub` uses,
same seed in every arm. Three interleaved repeats, medians:

| arm | fill wall s | fill seeks | gui seeks | gui wait p95 ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 reader, nobody scrubbing | 1.761 | 2 | — | — |
| 2 readers, nobody scrubbing | 1.761 | 2 | — | — |
| 1 reader, live hand | **11.353** | 6 | 18 | 0.07 |
| 2 readers, live hand | **1.992** | 2 | 5 | 15.2 |

**A person costs the fill 6.4× with one cursor and 13% with two.** 1.761 s
parked, 11.353 s with a hand on it, 1.992 s once the hand has its own cursor.
The pre-registered outcome was that the fill wall falling toward the parked
cost means the overlap is real and collectible; it falls to within a seventh of
a second of it.

**The fill's seek count is the mechanism, and it is exact.** Two seeks parked,
two seeks with two readers, and 6 with one — plus 18 the GUI caused on the same
cursor. Under a partition the sweep is a perfect sequential reader whatever the
hand does, which is what `a-second-cursor-makes-preemption-free` measured on V1
and this reproduces on a different implementation.

**The second reader costs nothing when it is idle.** Parked, one reader and two
are the same wall to the millisecond (1.761 against 1.761), because the extra
reader opens its container and then sits: it serves only INTERACTIVE picks and
there are none. The contention that
`2026.08.21-software-decoders-collapse-under-contention` measured for four
concurrent software workers does not appear, for the same reason it did not
appear in V1 — the second reader is not concurrent with the first except when
somebody is waiting.

**This is a bigger number than the decode budget predicted, and the two do not
disagree.** That finding put the unclaimed overlap at about a third of a filled
window, measured on V1 *already running `--gui-cursor`*, which removes the
cursor-stealing but not the serialisation. The 6.4× here is both defects at
once: `readers=1` has neither mitigation, so it pays the seek pairs *and* the
serialisation. The overlap alone is the difference the earlier finding named;
the rest is the cursor.

**What two readers cost the person is a tail, and it is small.** GUI wait p95
goes from 0.07 ms to 15.2 ms, because with one cursor the fill is abandoned
instantly for every interactive pick, and with two the person's own cursor
sometimes has to seek. Trading 15 ms of tail for 9.4 seconds of fill is not a
close call. The p50 is 0.05–0.06 ms in both arms: most requested rows are
already resident, since the hand is scrubbing inside the window being filled.

## What this does not establish

**Anything felt.** A scripted gaussian walk is not a hand, and a wall is not
how a scrub feels. The explorer is where that is decided, and this measures the
mechanism the explorer will be built on.

**The GUI latency comparison is not like for like.** The one-reader arm runs
5.7× longer, so its hand ticks 5.7× more often and shows more frames — 64
against 15. The percentiles are per-sample and comparable; the counts are not,
and neither is `stale` (29 against 6) which scales with how long the arm ran.

**Nothing about more than two.** One reader per band, two bands. Whether a
third helps is unasked, and
`2026.08.21-software-decoders-collapse-under-contention` is the reason to
expect not: four concurrent software decoders had aggregate throughput below
one. The arrangement that survives there is asymmetric — software for the
sweep, hardware for the interactive cursor — and nothing here is hardware.

**One window, one form, no step, one machine.** 160 frames at full frame; no
tool running, because a step would make the decode counts a statement about
scheduling as well as about cursors. The one-reader arm's spread is wide
(9.4–15.1 s) and that is not noise: the wall is linear in role alternations and
the walk produces a different number of them each time, which
`the-pressure-dispatcher-preempts-into-seeks` established. The two-reader arm's
spread is 1.9–2.16.

## How to recreate

```
uv run --group tools --group experiments python experiments/orchestrator2-experiments/06-two-readers.py
```

The parked arms are the floor the live arms are read against; running only the
live pair says two readers are faster without saying that they have reached the
cost of nobody scrubbing at all.
