---
title: Holding a chained step's field pays above a producer cost of ~0.3 ms, and not below
measured: 2026-08-30
where: experiments/chain-experiments/results/02-chained-field-20260830T200535Z.json, and a second run beside it
status: measured in this tree (v4), at the small 462² regime, on a sequential scan
---

A step fed another step's field has to get that field from somewhere, and
nothing image-sized is durable in this tree. Recomputed, a consumer admitting
lags 30/20/10 pays four of the producer's fields per row. Held under ADR-0006's
release rule — drop everything below `row - reach` — it pays about one, and
holds `reach + 1` fields while it does.

`lk flow` over `rep3_intermittent_crop.MP4` (462×456, h264), 240 consumer rows,
p50 ms per consumer row:

| | p50 | p95 | producer's share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| recomputed | 9.415 | 11.58 | 8.89 |
| held | 3.852 | 6.40 | 3.33 |
| consumer's arithmetic alone | 0.527 | 0.77 | — |

Holding costs 31 fields, 26.1 MB at this form, and changes no number: held and
recomputed agree to 0.0 across all 240 rows.

**The reuse ratio overstates the win, and the floor is why.** The consumer asks
for each upstream field 3.56 times over this sweep — 960 demands of 270 distinct
rows, approaching 4 as the sweep grows — but wall time improves only 2.44×,
because 0.527 ms/row is the consumer's own four maximums and no cache touches
it. Read on the producer's share alone the ratio is 2.67×, which is what
1.125 fields per row against 4 predicts. A chain's cost floor is the sum of its
steps' own arithmetic, and caching moves everything except that.

**Which gives a crossover, and it is low enough to matter.** At 2.22 ms per
producer field, holding removes 2.875 fields per row and pays about 0.83 ms/row
of bookkeeping and residency for it. Below roughly **0.29 ms per producer
field**, holding is a pessimization: it does the same arithmetic, keeps 26 MB
resident, and bills for the release. Against
`2026.08.21-optical-flow-dominates-the-analysis` at this same 462² regime, that
line falls between the reductions and the flows — absdiff (0.013) and
bgsub+count (0.039) are an order of magnitude below it and should recompute;
DIS-ultrafast (1.5), DIS-medium (8.8) and Farneback (33.9) are above it and
should not. The 0.83 ms/row overhead is a residual of two p50s rather than a
direct measurement, so treat the crossover as an order, not a threshold. A
second run of the same code puts the floor at 0.30 ms/row rather than 0.53 and
the crossover at 0.20 rather than 0.29 — the floor is the smallest number here
and the least stable, and everything derived from it inherits that.

## What this assumed

**Frames were resident in both cases, by construction.** Every frame the sweep
needed was read into RAM before either case was timed, so decode is not in any
of these numbers. Whether a frame cache pays is a settled question elsewhere
(`2026.08.30-derived-eviction-reproduces-the-fixed-window`) and leaving it to
vary here would have priced it again and buried the field question underneath.

**The sweep is sequential and ascending.** This is the series-fill shape, not
the tuning loop.

**The consumer's offsets are the whole source of the reuse.** 3.56× is a
property of lags 30/20/10 over 240 rows, not of fields.

**One form, one crop, one thread, no contention, one chain two deep.**
Sparse Lucas-Kanade, not dense flow; a float32 field at 462×456. cv2 5.0.0,
numpy 2.5.2, 32 logical CPUs, nothing else running. Machine and versions are in
the result.

## When it will not hold

- **A scrub.** A jump larger than the consumer's span evicts every held field,
  reuse falls to 1, and the hold becomes pure overhead until the span refills.
  The interactive loop is the scrub case and it is unmeasured here.
- **A cheap producer.** Below the crossover above, recomputing wins. This
  result is about a producer costing milliseconds, which is a property of
  optical flow rather than of chaining.
- **A shallow consumer.** A consumer admitting `(0,)` reuses nothing and a
  consumer at `(-1, 0)` reuses each field twice. The win scales with the
  consumer's declaration, so a chain of narrow steps gets none of this.
- **A deeper chain.** Depth two only. The floor adds up per step and the hold
  is per producer, so neither number extrapolates to depth three by
  multiplication.
- **A larger analysis form.** The same 31 fields at 5312×2988 float32 would be
  1.97 GB — an extrapolation by pixel count, not a measurement, and one that
  should not occur: `proxy_form` bounds a step's analysis size, so the field is
  held at the crop or the proxy form and never at source sampling. If a step
  ever measures at full source sampling, the hold is the first thing that
  stops being affordable.

## What it did not establish

That FIELD needs to be an edge kind. The refusals in `02`'s first check are
carried by the *want* being a declaration; the durable-key collision is closed
by the sample format being spelled in `Form.pix`. Neither needed a fourth kind —
a frame at `pix="f32"` would have failed both the same way. The kind's case is
that a field has different answers from a frame to the three questions
`contract/edges.py` says a kind owes the substrate — how it is stored, how it is
keyed, how it is drawn — and this run exercised keying only.

## How to recreate

```
uv run --group tools --group experiments python experiments/chain-experiments/02-chained-field.py
```

The two timed cases and the floor probe are `consumer-recomputed`,
`consumer-held` and `consumer-arithmetic-only`. The floor probe holds every
field with nothing released — 270 fields resident — which is not a proposal,
only the number the other two are read against.
