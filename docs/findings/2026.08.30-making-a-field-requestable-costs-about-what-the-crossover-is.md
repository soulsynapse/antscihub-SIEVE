`orchestrator2-experiments` README question 2 asked whether making a step's
output requestable — held in the refcounted pool under a key, delivered by the
dispatcher re-entering the consumer — costs anything on top of the plain
`Held` dict that `2026.08.30-holding-a-chained-field-pays-above-a-producer-
crossover` measured the hold with. It pre-registered both branches: free means
every node's output can be requestable with no second decision; measurable
means the crossover moves upward by the bookkeeping and "requestable" becomes
a choice with a threshold. **It is the second.**

Two-deep chain, DIS-ultrafast producer offering its field, consumer at lags
30/20/10 plus 0 over that field, 120 consumer rows, every frame resident
before timing so decode is in none of it. Three interleaved repeats:

| arm | chain ms per consumer row, per repeat | median |
|---|---|---:|
| recomputed | 6.95 / 5.60 / 7.52 | 6.95 |
| held (`binding.Held`) | 2.14 / 2.28 / 2.96 | 2.28 |
| dispatched, 1 recorder | 2.50 / 3.19 / 3.03 | 3.03 |
| dispatched, 2 recorders | 2.51 / 3.44 / 3.14 | 3.14 |

All four arms agree on every value, 0 rows disagreeing at a maximum absolute
difference of 0.0.

**The dispatcher costs about 0.75 ms per consumer row over the dict**, and it
is dispatched-slower-than-held in all three repeats individually, not only in
the medians. Both arms compute the same 150 producer fields over the same 120
consumer rows, so the difference is bookkeeping and not work. Spread over the
270 activations a repeat performs — 150 producer, 120 consumer — that is
**0.33 ms per activation**, which is the same number
`2026.08.30-re-entry-removes-the-poll-interval-and-not-the-wall` measured as
the activation handoff (0.29 ms p50) on a different experiment, a different
tool and a different footage.

**Which is why the answer is a threshold and not a policy.** The chained-field
finding puts the crossover for holding a producer's field at roughly 0.3 ms of
producer cost. The dispatcher's own per-activation overhead is that same
figure. So routing a hold through it costs about what the hold's entire
justification is worth: for a producer above the crossover by a wide margin it
disappears, and for one near the crossover it eats the win. A frame is the
easy case — a decode is 6–10 ms and 0.33 ms of bookkeeping is noise, which is
why the pool earns its place for frames without anyone having to check. A
chain of cheap steps is the opposite case, and making each intermediate
requestable would spend more on the machinery than the arithmetic it saves.

That is a measured argument for the separation `sieve/pipeline/binding.py`
already asserts. Its `Held` says it is "not to be grown into a pool" and that
composite keys, a byte budget and sharing counts belong to the pool instead;
the two are now known to be different tools for different cost regimes rather
than one tool spelled twice.

**A second recorder buys nothing here** — 3.14 against 3.03 — because request
depth is 1, so there is never more than one activation of a node in flight and
nothing to overlap. The re-entry finding measured the same knob doing nothing
for the same reason in the opposite regime.

## What this is not

**Not `requestable` in VapourSynth's sense.** Demand does not propagate
backwards in this experiment: the producer is driven by its own `Pass`, and a
consumer asking for a field row *waits* for it rather than *causing* it.
VapourSynth's `requestFrameFilter` propagates through the DAG, which is the
half that makes a pull graph a pull graph. What was measured is a push
pipeline sharing a keyed pool, so "requestable" here means "held and served
under a key". Whether propagation costs more than this is unmeasured, and it
cannot cost less.

**Not a re-derivation of the crossover.** The producer-cost line below which
holding a chained field is a pessimisation is the chained-field finding's,
measured with a different producer, and it is cited rather than recomputed.
What is new here is that the dispatcher's overhead sits at the same order as
that line.

**Not a decode measurement.** Every frame was resident before any arm was
timed, by construction, as in the finding this reads against.

**One chain, two deep, one form, one crop, one machine.** DIS ultrafast at
462×456; the consumer's 3.56× reuse is a property of lags 30/20/10 over these
rows. Depth three does not follow by multiplication, and neither does a
different producer.

**An earlier run of this experiment reported the opposite.** It timed only the
consumer's activation in the dispatched arms — with the producer's fields
already in the pool from a separate pass — and compared that floor against the
other arms' totals, making the dispatcher look four times faster than a dict.
The per-row samples in the result are still that floor and are still useful as
one; `chain_ms_per_consumer_row` is the comparable figure and the table above
is built from it.

## How to recreate

```
uv run --group tools --group experiments python experiments/orchestrator2-experiments/02-requestable.py
```

`REPS` interleaves the arms; reading one repeat of each is how this experiment
and the re-entry one both went wrong first.
