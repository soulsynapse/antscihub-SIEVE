`orchestrator2-experiments` README question 1 pre-registered both outcomes: a
foreground latency that moves makes re-entry a performance result, and one
that does not makes it a structural one. On a window fill with nothing else
declaring, it is the structural one — and the term re-entry actually deletes
is real, large, and entirely hidden.

Three arms, interleaved, three repeats each, over 160 full-frame rows with a
sweep declaring the window and one step at reach 1 riding along. The arms
differ only in how the step learns its inputs arrived:

| arm | handoff p50 | p95 | wall, per repeat | threads per step |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| polling (5 ms, 10 s deadline) | 2.823 | 5.224 | 1.423 / 1.545 / 1.671 | 1 |
| re-entry, request depth 1 | 0.291 | 0.365 | 1.547 / 1.570 / 1.772 | 0 |
| re-entry, request depth 16 | 0.422 | 0.545 | 1.538 / 1.604 / 1.600 | 0 |

Handoff is milliseconds from the last input a row needs becoming resident to
that row's arithmetic starting, sampled per row (n=477 per arm) against one
clock that neither arm owns — every landing is stamped by the pool, so no arm
reports its own latency.

**The handoff is the poll interval, and nothing else.** 2.823 ms p50 against
a 5 ms poll is the mean wait for a uniformly-arriving frame; 5.224 ms p95 is
the interval itself. Re-entry replaces it with a condition wakeup and a queue,
at 0.291 ms. The ratio is 9.7×, and every one of the three repeats agrees.
A second full run of the same script, after the dispatcher gained a reader
count it did not use, put the three arms at 2.612 / 0.280 / 0.405 — the same
result to the precision it is claimed at.

**The wall does not move.** All nine runs fall in 1.42–1.77 s and the arms are
not separable inside that: each arm is slower in the third repeat than in the
first, by more than the arms differ from each other, so the session drifts
faster than the effect. Decode is identical in every run — 160 served, 1 seek,
159 steps — and the values agree exactly, 0 rows disagreeing at a maximum
absolute difference of 0.0.

The reason is that the handoff was never on the critical path here. The
polling arm spends roughly 0.4 s of total handoff across the window, and all
of it overlaps decode the fetch thread was doing anyway: the fetch thread is
the bottleneck, and it never waits on the step. Deleting the waiting deletes
waiting that cost nothing. **Which is why this is a structural result, and
where to look for a felt one:** the handoff is only visible when something is
waiting on the row, and the only consumer that waits is the one with a person
behind it. That measurement is the explorer's and is not made here.

**A single run of each arm said the opposite, and was wrong.** It read
polling at 1.400 s against re-entry at 1.607 s and the difference as a cost
of re-entry. Interleaved repeats show that gap is drift. A probe timing
`Graph.pressure_queue` directly — the mechanism proposed for the imagined
cost — found it rising with depth as predicted (81.5 µs per pick at depth 1,
244.2 µs at depth 16) but totalling 13 ms against 40 ms across the whole
window, an eighth of the gap it was supposed to explain. The mechanism was
real and the effect was not.
`2026.08.30-the-remaining-wall-is-decode-and-a-reader-that-does-not-overlap`
reached the same place independently and measured it better, at 0.234 ms of
bookkeeping per decode against a hypothesis two orders of magnitude larger.
Reading `pressure_queue` and concluding it must be the hot spot is evidently
a mistake worth making only once more.

**Request depth buys nothing and costs a little.** Depth is this folder's own
knob — how many activations of one node may be in flight — and it exists
because the activation model permits what a thread could not. Deeper raises
the handoff (0.291 to 0.422 p50) because ready activations queue behind each
other, and moves the wall not at all. With a step whose arithmetic is about
3% of its row's decode, the recorder threads are nearly idle and there is
nothing for depth to overlap. Depth 1 is the setting; the deep arm is kept
and reported rather than deleted, because "deeper is worse here" is a result
and an unmeasured knob with one value is not.

**`starved` never fired in either arm.** The polling arm's ten-second
deadline was not reached once, so its absence under re-entry is a structural
claim — there is no deadline to exceed — and not an improvement observed
here. The same holds for the thread count: the polling arm runs one thread
per step, the re-entry arms none, and neither number was measured against a
machine that ran out of threads.

## What this assumed

**Nothing else declared.** No GUI, no second window, no contention: the whole
window is one sequential run, 1 seek in 160 rows in every arm. That is the
quiet case on purpose — the arms were chosen to differ in one thing, and
adding a preempting consumer would have made the handoff a statement about
preemption as well.

**One reader.** The dispatcher ran a single fetch thread here, which is V1's
shape and what makes these walls comparable to V1's. What a second cursor
does is a different question, already answered for preemption in
`2026.08.30-a-second-cursor-makes-preemption-free`, and untouched by this.

**One tool, one form, one window size, one machine.** absdiff at reach 1,
arithmetic around 0.3 ms against a ~9 ms decode; the full luma plane at source
sampling with the step deriving its own crop; 160 rows. A step whose
arithmetic approaches its decode cost is where depth could pay and is
unmeasured.

**Both arms ran the same dispatcher.** The polling arm is
`orchestrator-experiments/explorer.py`'s `ToolRunner._work` with Qt removed
and nothing else changed, driven by the same fetch thread, same pool, same
graph, same pressure queue. What differs is the step's arrangement and not
the machinery underneath it.

## How to recreate

```
uv run --group tools --group experiments python experiments/orchestrator2-experiments/01-reentry.py
```

Every handoff sample is kept, per arm, across all repeats; `walls_s_per_repeat`
in each case's params is what makes the drift visible. Reading one repeat of
each arm is the mistake this finding documents.
