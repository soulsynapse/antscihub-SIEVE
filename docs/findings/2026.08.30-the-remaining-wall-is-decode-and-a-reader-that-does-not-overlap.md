`2026.08.30-a-second-cursor-makes-preemption-free` took a 480-frame window
from 24–33 s to 6.1–7.0 s and left the obvious question unasked. This is what
the 6.7 s is. One leg, live, under `--gui-cursor`:

| term | s | of wall | per frame |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| fill decodes | 4.28 | 64% | 9.60 ms × 446 |
| GUI decodes | 2.17 | 32% | 63.8 ms × 34 |
| everything else | 0.27 | 4% | — |
| **wall** | **6.72** | | |

The three legs agree: 3.90/2.25/0.27, 4.28/2.17/0.27, 4.34/1.42/0.30.

**The wall is `fill + gui`, not `max(fill, gui)`.** The second cursor bought
the sweep's locality and no concurrency: there is one dispatcher thread, and
`reader.exact()` blocks it whichever container it is holding. So the GUI's
2.17 s is serial addition. That is the largest single item on the sheet and
the one the arrangement was not built to collect.

**The fill is close to this machine's decode floor.** Sequential luma off the
same file, one reader, one thread, nothing held, 400 frames after a 40-frame
warm-up:

| case | p50 | mean | marginal |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| decode only | 5.789 | 6.991 | — |
| decode + luma view | 6.218 | 7.647 | +0.656, the numpy wrapper |
| decode + luma copy | 6.491 | 7.964 | +0.317, the memcpy |
| the copy alone, off a held frame | 3.663 | 3.576 | — |

So 446 frames at 7.964 is 3.55 s of the fill's 4.28 s, and the gap is 0.73 s
— 1.64 ms/frame of pool, envelope, graph and three threads in one process.
Of that gap, the graph is 0.11 s: the dispatcher's whole per-decode
bookkeeping, `pressure_queue` plus `unserved` over a 480-position declaration
plus `still_wants` plus the GUI's `declare`, is 0.234 ms. That was the first
hypothesis for the gap and it is wrong by two orders of magnitude —
`pressure_queue` sorts every need and builds a set of every declared position
twice per call, after every decode, and reading the code it is obviously the
hot spot. It is not.

**The copy is free because it is hiding, not because it is cheap.** 0.317 ms
in situ against 3.576 ms off a held frame is 11×, and it is not cache: 15.9 MB
in 0.317 ms is 50 GB/s, above this machine's DRAM. The decoder is
multithreaded and runs ahead, so the copy overlaps codec threads that are the
critical path. It costs ~3.6 ms of CPU and ~0.32 ms of wall, and the second
number holds only while decode is the bottleneck. Anything that stops decode
being the bottleneck — a second reader competing for those threads most of
all — should expect to find it again.

**What is available, in order.** Against 6.72 s: overlapping the two readers
is ~2.1 s (32%), the fill's non-decode gap is 0.73 s (11%), and the whole
plane-extraction path is 0.47 s (7%). The floor for one software reader is
480 × 6.991 ms = 3.36 s. `--abandon-within 480` already reaches 4.6–5.6 s by
declining to serve the GUI at all, which is the fill-only wall and the
evidence that the fill term is real rather than an artifact of sharing.

**The overlap cannot be two software decoders.**
`2026.08.21-software-decoders-collapse-under-contention` measured four
software workers at 29–30 fps each with aggregate *below* one worker alone,
and concluded decoder-per-consumer is dead on arrival. The shape that
survives there is the asymmetric pair, 1sw+1hw at 186 fps aggregate with
NVDEC holding 100–109 fps under every shape. The split this result found is
the same split: the GUI reader's cost is 6 seeks ≈ 1.8 s of its 2.17 s, and
`2026.08.21-uncut-seek-costs-a-gop-not-a-frame` has NVDEC losing sequentially
and winning on seek latency, while the fill is sequential throughout at
`fill:seek` of 1 per 480. Software for the sweep, hardware for the
interactive cursor, is the arrangement both measurements point at
independently. **It is a lead, not a result — nothing here ran two readers
concurrently.**

## What this assumed

**One reader at a time, one thread, no Qt, no tool, no pool.** Every case in
the terms table is isolated by construction. That is what makes them
subtractable and it is also why none of them is a number about the loop: the
folder's rule is that a claim about the loop is taken in the loop, and the
budget line above is subtraction over an explorer log, printed and never
stored.

**`thread_type = "AUTO"`, and the machine has 32 logical CPUs.** The decode
floor is a floor for a decoder that has the machine to itself. Every
conclusion about the copy hiding depends on that.

**One file.** 5312×2988 HEVC, 23.976 fps, GOP 24, the uncut source. Not the
crop the tool reads and not a proxy.

**The budget leg is one leg of one log.** The three legs agree to 0.4 s on the
fill term and vary by 0.8 s on the GUI term, which is the interaction the
walk scripts differing between them.

## What would invalidate it

- **A decoder that is not thread-starved.** If the codec threads stop being
  the critical path — a smaller form, a faster decoder, a second consumer
  taking the cores — the 0.317 ms copy becomes something nearer the 3.576 ms
  the cold case measures, and the plane-extraction term stops being 7% of the
  wall. The two numbers are both in the result file so the substitution can be
  made without re-measuring.
- **Two readers actually running concurrently.** The 32% overlap figure
  assumes the GUI's 2.17 s disappears entirely under `max()`. If the
  asymmetric pair degrades the software worker at all — and `07-contention`
  says a second consumer degrades the first by a measurable factor — the
  recovered time is less, and the sw+hw shape has never been run inside this
  dispatcher.
- **A different machine.** The floor is 6.991 ms/frame here against the
  5.3–6 ms/frame `2026.08.21-sequential-luma-ceiling-is-shared` measured on a
  22-CPU, RTX 4060 box. Neither supersedes the other; a floor is a property of
  hardware and the two are different hardware. Anything reading a percentage
  off this finding on a third machine should re-run `08-decode-budget.py`
  first — it takes seconds and writes its own machine block.
- **The gap being contention rather than work.** The fill's 1.64 ms/frame
  above isolated decode is attributed by subtraction, not by a clock. Three
  threads share this process — Qt, the tool polling at 5 ms, the dispatcher —
  and if that gap is mostly scheduling then it is not 0.73 s of removable
  work, it is the cost of the other two consumers existing.

## Where it does not hold

- **Any window that is not covered.** Every number here is per *filled*
  window. The interactive case that matters most — a scrub into a region
  nothing has decoded — is a different question and is measured nowhere;
  `2026.08.30-holding-a-chained-field-pays-above-a-producer-crossover` flags
  the same gap for the held-field policy.
- **At a smaller form.** The tool reads a 1024² crop and the GUI draws at
  display size. Nothing in the pipeline actually wants 5312×2988 luma except
  as a dominating plane, and
  `2026.08.21-sequential-luma-ceiling-is-shared` measured that display-sized
  pushdown through libavfilter is free. At that form the decode floor, the
  copy and the allocator pressure are all different numbers and the ordering
  above may invert.
- **With the analysis attached.** These legs run `absdiff`, and
  `2026.08.21-optical-flow-dominates-the-analysis` puts DIS-medium at 8.8 ms
  and Farneback at 33.9 ms per frame at 462². A window whose tool is Farneback
  is not decode-bound and none of this is where its wall goes.
- **As a statement about the product.** `serve`, `fill` and `proxy` in `src/`
  hold the fixed reader assignment; the dispatcher measured here is an
  experiment module. 480 frames is 20.0 s of footage, so 6.72 s is 3.0×
  realtime and the interactive constraint is already met — the headroom is
  what a heavier step gets to spend, not a stall being fixed.

## How to recreate

```
uv run --group experiments python experiments/orchestrator-experiments/08-decode-budget.py
```

Takes the newest live `--gui-cursor` log for the budget subtraction, or
`--log <name>` to name one. The terms are independent of any log and run
without one.
