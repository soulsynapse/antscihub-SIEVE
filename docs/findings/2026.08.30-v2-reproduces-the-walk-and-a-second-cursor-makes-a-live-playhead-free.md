Two things at once, because one is the precondition for reading the other.
`orchestrator2-experiments` owes a reproduction of V1's walk before V1 can
retire, and that reproduction is also the baseline the reader count is measured
against. Full-size windows, 480 frames, the same five legs under V1's names:

| readers | live playhead | leg 2 A cold | leg 4 B seam | leg 5 A again | seeks | stale | expired picks |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | no | 6.576 | 6.777 | 6.361 | 33 | 15 | 16 |
| 2 | no | 4.378 | 4.745 | 4.691 | 31 | 16 | 1 |
| 1 | yes | 7.117 | **19.626** | 9.668 | 84 | 66 | 32 |
| 2 | yes | 4.473 | 4.797 | 4.860 | 30 | 21 | 1 |

**The reproduction holds.** At one reader with the playhead parked — V1's
shape — the walls are 6.576 / 6.777 / 6.361 against
`2026.08.30-the-pressure-dispatcher-preempts-into-seeks`'s parked condition of
6.6 / 6.6 / 6.3, and the per-leg seeks are 8 / 7 / 6 against its 9 / 7 / 6. A
different implementation of the same scheduling contract, walking the same
script, lands on the same numbers. That is what V1's retirement was made
conditional on.

**A second cursor takes about a third off every leg even with nobody
scrubbing.** 4.378 / 4.745 / 4.691 against 6.576 / 6.777 / 6.361. That is the
term `2026.08.30-the-remaining-wall-is-decode-and-a-reader-that-does-not-
overlap` priced at about a third of a filled window and could not collect,
because V1 has one dispatcher thread that blocks in `exact()` whichever
container it holds. Two measurements, two implementations, one number.

**With two cursors a live playhead is free.** Live against parked at two
readers is 4.473 / 4.797 / 4.860 against 4.378 / 4.745 / 4.691 — within 4%,
which is less than this machine drifts. At one reader the same comparison is
7.117 / 19.626 / 9.668 against 6.576 / 6.777 / 6.361, and leg 4 nearly triples.
`a-second-cursor-makes-preemption-free` established that a live playhead costs
what a parked one costs once the sweep's cursor stops being taken; this
reproduces it and adds the overlap on top, so the two-reader live walk is
faster than the *one-reader parked* walk by a third.

**The deadline is load-bearing exactly where predicted and idle everywhere
else.** `expired_picks` is 32 in the one-reader live walk, 16 one-reader
parked, and **1** in both two-reader walks. The expiry queue
(`05-starvation.py`) exists because ranking for locality starves whoever ranks
last; a second cursor removes the contention that causes the starvation, so
the mechanism stops firing rather than continuing to pay for itself. A run
where it fires often is a run where something was being starved.

**And it is why the one-reader live walk is not as bad as V1's.** V1's live
condition measured 34.7 / 22.5 / 11.3 under the derived rank; V2's one-reader
live walk is 7.117 / 19.626 / 9.668. The deadline is the difference — 32 picks
the ranking would never have made — and it is the half of anticipatory
scheduling V1 carried without.

## What this does not establish

**Anything from repetition.** One walk per cell. The two-reader cells agree
with each other across the live/parked boundary, which is some evidence of
stability, but leg 4 at one reader with a live playhead is a single sample of
a quantity V1 showed to be wildly variable — six controls of its identical
script spanned 7.8 to 33.3 s, because the wall is linear in role alternations
and the script produces a different number of them each run. **19.626 should be
read as "the bad case is bad", not as a figure.** The two-reader cells are the
ones worth quoting, because their spread across four legs and two conditions is
under half a second.

**Nothing felt.** This is a scripted walk and a wall is not how a scrub feels.
Leg 2's scrub p95 falls from 13.66 ms at one reader to 0.07 ms at two, and
hunt p50 sits at 247–277 ms in every cell — the cold-seek band — but a person
has not driven this explorer yet.

**Nothing about the tool's share.** One tool at a time, swapped once in leg 3,
and the duration bars split `dispatch:fill` / `dispatch:gui` / the step without
anyone yet asking whether that split is the one the pipeline cards should show.

**One machine, one file, one window size, one crop.** And `refetched_predicted`
reads 2 across the walk against ADR-0008's target of zero, which is close
enough to clean to be worth watching rather than acting on.

## How to recreate

```
uv run --group tools --group experiments python experiments/orchestrator2-experiments/explorer.py --walk [--readers 2] [--live-playhead]
```

`--live-playhead` is the condition the reader comparison has to be run in:
without it the walk scrubs and then waits for coverage, so the hand is idle for
exactly the stretch being timed and there is nothing for a second cursor to
overlap with. `explorer-logs/README.md` carries the field-presence test for
telling these logs from ones written before the walk worked.
