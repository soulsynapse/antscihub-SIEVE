ADR-0017 says a frame fetched to be looked at is reduced to display sampling
before it is copied or held, and source sampling is kept for what will be
recorded. Nothing in `orchestrator2-experiments` had done it: one form, full
luma at source sampling, every consumer reading the same array. Doing it puts
a 20-second window at **0.443 GB where it was 7.095 GB** for **59 more
decodes out of 1477** — and costs 9.4 s over three legs of the walk, in three
terms that account for all of it: twenty more seeks, and most of them mid-GOP;
the resampling that makes the coarse form; and the sequential decodes the step
adds where the fill's run no longer covers it.

The whole result is that a second form is free exactly while the step is
declaring what the fill is about to decode, and that two different things
break that.

## What makes it one decode

A decode produces the source plane whatever form asked for it, so every other
form of that row is a build over bytes already in hand. `Dispatcher(tiers=...)`
puts each one that `graph.wanter` says is already declared and unserved.
Restricted that way, a row the two consumers want at the same moment costs one
decode and a row they want at different moments costs two — and the counters
split exactly on that: for every row not co-served, one decode finds the other
tier *undeclared* (the fill got there first) and one finds it *present* (the
step arriving later at a row the fill already served). `cofetched +
coserve_present + coserve_undeclared` is the decode count in every arm.

## Parked, the lever is order

160 rows, one reader, two recorders, `absdiff` at the 1024² crop, DropAll,
three interleaved repeats, medians. `follows` walks the step over the sweep's
attention-first rotation instead of ascending:

| arm | decodes | co-served | fill s | step s | held GB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| source-only (one tier) | 160 | — | 1.917 | 2.073 | 2.365 |
| two-tier, depth 1 | 240 | 80 | 2.076 | 2.807 | 0.177 |
| two-tier, depth 32 | 240 | 80 | 1.961 | 2.663 | 0.177 |
| two-tier, depth 1, follows | **160** | **160** | 1.945 | **1.956** | 0.170 |
| two-tier, depth 1, no co-serve | 320 | 0 | 1.980 | 3.755 | 0.177 |

Two tiers with the orders matched cost **nothing**: one decode per row, the
step finishing the same instant the fill does, at a fourteenth of the held
bytes. Without the co-serve it is exactly two decodes per row. Depth changes
nothing here — 1 and 32 are the same number, and the 80 they both leave on the
floor is the count of rows between the sweep's anchor and the end of its
window, which is where an ascending step and an attention-first fill disagree.

**The seam has to move.** A step with reach cannot simply take the rotation:
`absdiff` at the anchor reads the row *below* the anchor, which the rotation
reaches last, so that activation waits for the whole window and a pass that
advances on completion never rejoins the frontier. Deferring it to the back is
enough. Deferring on *what has been placed so far* instead of on position in
the rotation cascades from the seam and unrotates the whole order, which
measures as if nothing had been done.

## Under a person, the lever is depth

The same window with `06-two-readers`' hand on it — one INTERACTIVE row at a
time on a gaussian random walk that re-anchors — at two readers, which is V2's
core shape for anything with a person in it:

| arm | decodes | co-served | fill s | step s | held GB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| source-only | 160 | — | 2.191 | 2.327 | 2.365 |
| two-tier, follows, depth 1 | 315 | 5 | 2.261 | 3.699 | 0.177 |
| two-tier, follows, depth 8 | 198 | 122 | 2.327 | 3.088 | 0.263 |
| two-tier, follows, depth 32 | **162** | **158** | 2.289 | **2.729** | 0.227 |

Order stops being sufficient the moment anything else touches the cursor: at
depth 1 the co-serve falls to 5 of 315. The frontier and the step separate,
and the request depth is what spans the lag — at 32 it is back to one decode a
row, and it costs `depth` source rows held, which is the 0.05 GB between the
depth-1 and depth-32 rows.

**This supersedes the request depth of 1** that
`2026.08.30-re-entry-removes-the-poll-interval-and-not-the-wall` settled. That
was measured under one form, where a step's rows *are* the fill's rows and
depth can only buy overlap it already has. It is not wrong; it is about a
different arrangement.

At one reader the same comparison is unreadable — the person and the fill
share a cursor, the fill wall goes to 15.9 s, and the arms differ by less than
they vary between repeats. That arrangement is the one
`2026.08.30-a-second-cursor-that-overlaps-costs-a-scrub-nothing` already
rejected, and it is reported in the result rather than dropped.

## The driven walk, and where the wall goes

The five legs at full size, 480-frame window, two readers, depth 32, software
sweep and CUDA interactive route. `--display-divisor 1` is one tier:

| | decodes | seeks | held GB | leg 2 / 4 / 5 s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| one tier, depth 1 | 1449 | 37 | 7.095 | 4.233 / 4.594 / 4.560 |
| one tier, depth 32 | 1418 | 37 | 7.095 | 4.232 / 4.627 / 4.665 |
| two tiers 1/4, depth 32 | 1477 | 57 | **0.443** | 6.841 / 6.989 / 9.094 |

The decode count barely moves (1418 → 1477; the co-serve carries 1414 of
them). The wall does, by 9.4 s over three legs, and the trace says what of:

| | seeks | sum s | median ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| one tier — `fill` | 3 | 1.19 | 422.1 |
| one tier — `gui` | 34 | 3.20 | 61.2 |
| two tiers — `fill` | 11 | 3.55 | 302.7 |
| two tiers — `gui` | 31 | 2.95 | 63.9 |
| two tiers — `tool` | **15** | **3.56** | 232.8 |

`tool` seeks do not exist under one tier, because the step never decodes
anything there. Under two it decodes the rows the co-serve missed, and each is
a mid-GOP seek at 233 ms against the GUI's keyframe-snapped 61 ms — the
distinction ADR-0018 bought for a drag and ADR-0019 targets for everything.
The sweep's own seeks go 3 → 11 as its run is fragmented by the step
interleaving into it, and the GUI's are unchanged.

Seeking costs 5.67 s more in total (10.06 against 4.39), resampling 2.19 s,
and stepped decodes 1.94 s more (14.45 against 12.51, over 39 more of them).
That is 9.8 s against a 9.4 s gap — the arms are one run each, so the terms
over-account by about a leg's noise, but nothing is left over for a term
nobody has named.

Both new terms are **two sequential runs at different forms on one cursor**,
which is the shape a reader per band already solves for a different pair. A
reader per *tier* is the un-built move the seek count points at.

## The divisor is 4, and it is not obvious

Same walk at each sampling, depth 32:

| divisor | held GB | resample ms/decode | leg 2 / 4 / 5 s |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1/2 | 1.774 | 1.13 | 7.292 / 6.900 / 8.452 |
| 1/4 | 0.443 | 1.48 | 6.841 / 6.989 / 9.094 |
| 1/8 | 0.111 | 7.29 | 8.508 / 10.673 / 10.001 |

1/8 holds four times less than 1/4 and is slower on every leg, because
`cv2.INTER_AREA` costs more per output pixel as the reduction grows and loses
whatever fast path 1/2 and 1/4 take: measured off the fetch thread at 0.96,
0.53 and 5.57 ms for the three. So the sampling is not a free dial toward
less memory, and 1/4 wins on both axes.

## What this does not establish

**That the tier is built the way ADR-0017 says.** The ADR cites
`AVCodecContext.lowres`, which reduces inside the decoder at no cost, and
records that this codec does not offer it so the approximation is *striding
the decoded plane* — which is what `decode-experiments/explorer.py` does, at
`arr[::n, ::n]` before the copy. This is not that. It is `forms.build`'s
canonical crop-resize-convert, because `forms.py` fixes that construction so
two producers of one form agree in the low bits, and a strided array under the
display form's key would be a key that lies. The 2.2 s of resampling per walk
is the price of that choice, and whether a strided sampling should be its own
form with its own key is a question about the shared `forms.py` and is not
asked here.

**Anything about how it feels.** The walk is scripted. Every wall above is a
fill or a step, not a latency: the GUI's own seeks barely move (34 → 31, 61 →
64 ms), which says the person was not made to wait, but no felt session has
been driven on two tiers.

**Anything about a longer timeline.** The 16× is what ADR-0017 rejects source
sampling *for* — a budget too small to hold enough timeline for a playhead to
stay inside it — and this measures one window, not a playhead travelling
beyond one. What a pool holds when the person keeps moving is untested, and
it is the case the byte ceiling would finally become load-bearing in.

**Anything at more than one reader per band.** Both readers here decode both
tiers. The seek terms above are the argument for partitioning by tier and not
a measurement of one.

**Anything about `dis_flow` as against `absdiff`.** The headless arms are
`absdiff` throughout; the walk switches tools at leg 3, so its legs 4 and 5
carry the slower tool in both arms alike. Whether a step whose arithmetic
exceeds a decode can hold the frontier at any depth is not answered — depth 32
was enough for both here.

## How to recreate

```
uv run --group tools --group experiments python experiments/orchestrator2-experiments/09-display-sampling.py
uv run --group tools --group experiments python experiments/orchestrator2-experiments/explorer.py --walk
uv run --group tools --group experiments python experiments/orchestrator2-experiments/explorer.py --walk --display-divisor 1
```

`REPS` interleaves the arms; the machine drifts by more than the parked arms
differ. The scrubbed arms must be read at two readers — at one, the person and
the fill fight for the cursor and every arm is that fight.

A log written before this landed has no `topology.display_divisor` and no
`step`, and its walls are not comparable; `explorer-logs/README.md` carries the
fingerprints.
