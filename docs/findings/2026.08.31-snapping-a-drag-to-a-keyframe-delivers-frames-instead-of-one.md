measured: 2026-08-31
where: experiments/orchestrator2-experiments/explorer-logs/orchestrator2-20260831T0213*.json — one walk with `--no-snap`, one without; both full size, readers 2

ADR-0018 settles that a drag in progress asks for the keyframe at or before its
target rather than the target itself. This is what that is worth on the uncut
5.3K source, from the explorer's five legs run twice with nothing between the
arms but the flag.

Scrub events that actually reached the screen, per leg, with their latency:

| arm | leg 2 | leg 4 | leg 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| exact row | — | 2 at 508.8 ms | 1 at 319.2 ms |
| keyframe | 7 at 0.04 ms (p95 140.6) | 7 at 0.74 ms (p95 149.9) | 3 at 0.07 ms |

**The change is in how many arrive, not only in how fast.** Asking for the
exact row, a drag through unfilled ground put one or two frames on screen for a
whole leg, because each request was superseded by the next long before its
frame-accurate seek could land. Asking for the keyframe, three to seven arrive,
and the median arrives in well under a millisecond.

**The median is fast because the snap collapses a burst onto one row.** Every
drag position inside one group of pictures snaps to the same keyframe, so a
burst of pointer events is one request; the second and later ones find it
resident. Total superseded activations fall from 241 to 183 across the walk for
the same reason.

**The p95 is the honest cost of a cold snap** — around 140–150 ms, against the
320–500 ms an exact row cost in the same legs. That gap is the group-of-pictures
replay that `2026.08.21-uncut-seek-costs-a-gop-not-a-frame` attributes the seek
cost to: landing on a keyframe stops at the landing, where landing before an
arbitrary row decodes forward to reach it.

**It costs the fill nothing.** Window-covered walls are 4.284 / 4.754 / 4.717
against 4.289 / 4.770 / 4.811, and the fill's own seeks are three in both arms.
Total GUI seek time falls from 8.61 s to 6.41 s.

## What this does not establish

**One walk per arm.** The scrub-event counts are single digits per leg by
construction — a leg contains a few dozen pointer positions and most coalesce —
so these are latencies from a handful of samples each, and the claim worth
carrying is the shape (several immediate against one late) rather than any of
the figures.

**Nothing about a hand.** A scripted gaussian walk with `_scrubbing` held down
is not a person, and the arm exists because
`2026.08.30-one-cursor-blacks-out-playback-for-a-whole-window` was a driven
session's complaint that a scripted run had not reproduced.

**Nothing about the other candidates.** ADR-0018 rejects a built proxy and a
derived cut on build cost, not on served latency, and the decode battery has
both of them faster than this once they exist. What is measured here is the
arm that needs nothing built.

**One file, whose GOP is fixed at 24.** The snap distance is bounded by the
group length, so a file with long or variable groups would show a worse worst
case; `2026.08.21-keyframe-index-is-cheap-and-the-gop-is-fixed` measures the
group here and says the fixedness is the encoder's property rather than a
general one.

## How to recreate

```
uv run --group tools --group experiments python experiments/orchestrator2-experiments/explorer.py --walk
uv run --group tools --group experiments python experiments/orchestrator2-experiments/explorer.py --walk --no-snap
```

`gui.snapped` in the log counts requests the snap redirected, and
`topology.kf_snap` says which arm wrote it. The scrub and snap rows of each
leg's `by_task` are the table above.
