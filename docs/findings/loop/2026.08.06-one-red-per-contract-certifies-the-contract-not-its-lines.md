# One red per contract certifies the contract, not each of its lines

The sampling itself is right: 23 deliberate violations would be busywork, and
the five chosen edges did establish the thing most at risk — that contracts
written over packages with no contents still bind. The defect is the step from
sample to verdict. `Every contract goes red on one line` is true; the verdict
`contracts over empty packages are not vacuously true` is a claim about all of
them, drawn from five.

The cheap correction at review is not "test all 24". It is: list the entries
whose referent is absent from the tree — a forbidden module with no directory, a
layer naming a package that was never created — because absence is the only
condition under which an entry can be inert. Here that list had exactly one
member and it was inert.

The general shape, for the vacuity list: **a passing contract whose subject does
not exist**. It sits beside the guard-with-a-test-and-no-caller from the v2
audit — same failure, opposite side. There the production caller was missing;
here the thing being governed is.
