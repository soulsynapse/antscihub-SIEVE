# A mutation sweep enumerated by hand misses what it did not look for

The sweep was the right instrument and it worked: ten refusals that the
re-derivation table could not see because they had no v2 row
(`findings/loop/2026.08.07-a-re-derivation-table-certifies-v2s-coverage-not-v3s.md`)
were found by neutering them and watching the criterion stay green. What did not
survive review is the sentence the item wrote afterwards — "each of the module's
22 `raise` statements" — because the module has 24.

The two it missed are not an oversight of attention. The item was reopened for
untested *refusals*, so the worker enumerated the things that refuse: the
validators, the pattern checks, the duplicate-id guards. `Pipeline.node`'s
trailing `raise KeyError(node_id)` is not a refusal; it is a lookup failing. It
reads as control flow rather than as a contract, and it is a contract only
because the docstring above it says `Raises: KeyError`. So the enumeration
matched the reopening, and the reopening was narrower than the module.

The generalisation worth carrying forward is that a mutation sweep has two
halves and only one of them is mechanical. Neutering a guard and re-running a
criterion cannot lie. Deciding what counts as a guard is a judgment made by the
session whose blind spot is under test, and its output looks identical either
way — 22 mutants killed reads as complete coverage whether the denominator was
22 or 24. A review that re-runs the sweep must therefore generate the list
itself, from the source, by a pattern rather than by reading; the cost is one
regex and a few seconds of pytest per line.

The prediction that would refute this: if the two survivors had been guards the
worker recognised and deliberately excluded with a reason, this would be a
scoping decision rather than a blind spot. The item gives no reason, states the
count as a fact about the module, and the count is wrong.

## The denominator is now the worker's claim list, and it is still short (2026-08-08, review of 274278d)

`scripts/mutation_sweep.py` landed since this file was written, so the
mechanical half is no longer where the risk is: the harness refuses a
non-unique anchor, purges bytecode, restores bytes, and refuses a red baseline.
The judgment half is untouched, and it moved rather than closed. A worker no
longer says "each of the module's N `raise` statements"; it enumerates one
mutant per claim its new test file makes and reports the ratio. `274278d`
reported 5 killed / 0 survived over `gui/transport/proxy_cache.py`, and the
review re-ran all five and confirmed every one. The denominator is the honest
count of *its own claims*, which is exactly why it certifies nothing about the
module: `del self._entries[index] ==> pass` survives, because a re-put's
promotion is a sixth claim the file never made. Carried as
`todo/a-re-put-does-not-promote-the-frame-it-replaces.md`.

So the reading a later distillation should take from both halves of this file
is the same one under a new surface. A clean sweep is evidence that the claims
listed are pinned, never that the subject is; the review that re-runs the
worker's list has re-run the worker's blind spot too, and only mutants the
review chose itself say anything new. Of the three the review added here, one
was the survivor above, one an equivalent mutant (`and self._entries` in the
eviction loop, unreachable while `bytes` and `capacity` are both non-negative,
cf. `2026.08.08-an-equivalent-mutant-is-a-claim-about-every-reachable-state.md`),
and one a false KILLED off the harness's own separator, recorded in
`2026.08.08-a-crashing-test-command-is-indistinguishable-from-a-killed-mutant.md`.

## The survivor the review chose is closed, and the reading it supports is unchanged (2026-08-08, review of 2d6cad1)

`del self._entries[index] ==> pass` no longer survives: `2d6cad1` added the
sixth claim and the sweep the item carried now reports KILLED, re-run here and
also with `-k recache_promotes` alone, so the kill is the new test's and not
another case's. The paragraph above is left as written — it recorded what was
true of `274278d`, and the point it was making does not depend on the survivor
still being open.

What the closure does confirm is the asymmetry this file argues. The mutant that
found the gap was chosen by the review, not by the worker whose claim list was
complete on its own terms; the worker that closed it took the mutant from the
item rather than generating one, which is correct for a criterion and would have
been insufficient for a coverage claim. A review re-running only the mutants the
diff names has re-run the diff's blind spot; this one added
`self._bytes -= self._entries[index].sizeInBytes() ==> pass` and
`self._entries.move_to_end(index) ==> pass` of its own and both were killed.
