# A verbatim port carries the buried vocabulary past review

The rename in ADR-1 is names only, and names are what a reviewer checks:
`filter_id` became `tool_id`, `sieve.filters` became `sieve.tools`, and every
one of those landed. What did not land is the vocabulary the names are made of.
`core/types.py` explains `FrameCount` by saying warmup is counted in a filter's
own input frames, and cites `FilterSpec.warmup_frames` twice as the field the
non-negativity protects — a field that does not exist under that name in this
repo and never did. Three reviews read those lines.

The failure mode is specific to the shape of the work rather than to any
reviewer: a port's diff is against v2, so the prose that came over unchanged is
the part of the diff that looks correct. The check that catches it has to be
against the *tree*, not against the source it came from, which is why the gate
scans `src/sieve/` whole rather than the files an item touched.

The AST route v2 took would have missed five of the six. v2 was hunting string
literals — filter ids typed into widgets — so `_string_constants` was the right
reader there and is the wrong one here: a `#:` comment is not a node, and the
buried word survives in comments precisely because nothing executes them.
