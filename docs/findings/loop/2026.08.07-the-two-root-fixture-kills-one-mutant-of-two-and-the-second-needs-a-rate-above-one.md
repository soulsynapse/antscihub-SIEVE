# The two-root fixture kills one mutant of two and the second needs a rate above one

The previous finding's third probe ends on a number — the edgeless pair plans to
5 and to 1 under `min` — and the item written from it carried that number plus
the claim that the same graph fails "under a fold that ranged over `dag.order`,
which means the two roots must also be the two extremes of `need`". The second
half is where it goes wrong, and the sentence contains its own refutation: if
the roots *are* the extremes of `need`, then the maximum over `need` is a root's,
and folding over `dag.order` returns exactly what folding over `dag.roots`
returns. The condition stated as making the mutant die is the condition making it
equivalent.

What separates the two collections is the opposite property — a node below a
root wanting *more* than the root does. `_fold` walks backwards accumulating
`need_below.at_input_of(rate) + own`, and `at_input_of` divides, so the only
term that can shrink a requirement on the way to the source is a rate greater
than one. Decimation, the rate change the file already had, multiplies; it makes
`need` grow faster towards the root and so reinforces the equivalence rather
than breaking it. A frame-doubler is the whole of what was missing, and one is
enough: `settle5` behind it wants five of its frames, which is three of the
source's, while a bare `settle1` root beside it wants one. Three is neither the
largest number in `need` nor the smallest number over the roots.

The `output_rate` half failed for an unrelated and more general reason. Two
functions carry the same guard with the same message on the same input, and the
plan calls one of them first — so a refusal asserted at the entry point is
answered by the other function and stays green when its own line is deleted. The
symptom is invisible in a mutation sweep run against `build`: the mutant looks
killed, and what killed it is the twin. This is not specific to the window
arithmetic. Any pair of duplicated validations reachable from one call site has
it, and the check is the same each time — delete the line, and if the test still
passes, the test is asserting the other copy.
