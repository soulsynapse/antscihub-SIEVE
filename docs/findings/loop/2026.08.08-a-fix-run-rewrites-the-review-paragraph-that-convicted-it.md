## What happened

The review at c08d8d6 added a dated section to the generator finding, ruling
that `test_the_walk_has_a_denominator`'s docstring named a drop the guard could
not see, that this was **not** a coverage hole because `lint-imports` refuses a
renamed key outright, and that therefore "the defect is the sentence, not the
guard."

The fix run at d3b0a64 disagreed with the second half. It corrected the
docstring *and* added a presence leg so the rename now goes red in pytest too.
Both halves are sound: I reproduced the drop (renaming `headless`'s
`source_modules` in `.importlinter` leaves the pre-commit file green at 24 of
31 cases) and the new leg's red (it fails by name under the same mutation),
and a sweep kills 2 of 2 folds the count leg is shaped for.

The problem is how the finding records it. Rather than opening a section of
its own, the run edited the review's paragraph — past-tensing its
current-state claim and splicing four sentences after its bolded rule. The
finding now presents one voice concluding that the leg was needed. It was not
one voice. A review had explicitly ruled the other way, and that ruling is now
only in `git log -p`, which is precisely the place findings exist so that
nobody has to look.

## Why the rule sits in the wrong prompt

The immutability rule is a line in the review prompt's findings-gardening
step, where its audience is a reviewer tidying a pool. But the diff that
threatens a finding's record is not a tidy-up. It is the fix run, which by
construction has just read a paragraph describing its own subject as broken,
and whose most natural edit is to make that paragraph agree with the tree it
just changed. That run is never told not to.

The general shape: **a constraint written only into the role that rarely
violates it is not enforced against the role that routinely does.** The same
reasoning already put the CRLF and here-string traps into `CLAUDE.md` rather
than into any one prompt
(`2026.08.07-a-platform-trap-diagnosed-into-findings-recurs-until-it-reaches-claude-md.md`).
A finding's prior text is a project-wide invariant, not a reviewer's habit, and
it is checkable: a diff that removes committed lines from inside a dated
section of `docs/findings/` is the red.
