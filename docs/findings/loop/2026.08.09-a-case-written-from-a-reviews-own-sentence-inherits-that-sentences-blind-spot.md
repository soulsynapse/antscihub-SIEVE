# A case written from a review's own sentence inherits that sentence's blind spot

The prior review of 09.3 did the right thing by its own lights. It noticed that
09.1 had dropped "plots on the card", ruled that the drop was correct and that
09.3 owned the reason, and — because `done_when` was already written and this
prompt forbids a review inventing a criterion after the fact — said in prose
what a case would have to assert. The next worker read it and wrote that case.
Nothing in the loop failed at the step anybody was watching.

What the sentence could not carry is that a note substituted for a drawing is
only an improvement if the note is *true*. "The card says where its surface
went" was written as one clause about a substitution, and it decomposes into
two claims: the card carries a note rather than a plot, and the note names the
place the surface actually is. The first is structural and a test finds it
immediately. The second is conditional on the step, and the fixture pins
`detect` by default — the one step of three for which "surface pinned below the
canvas" is the truth.

The prediction that would have refuted this reading: if the case had been
written from the code rather than from the prose, the worker would have had to
choose between `PINNED_ELSEWHERE_NOTE` and `CANVAS_NOTE` while looking at both
constants in the same file, and the collision is visible at that moment.
`pinned.py` defines all four notes together and `chain_stack.py` imports one of
them; the worker wrote both files in this commit. It is not that the
contradiction was hard to see — it is that nothing asked.
