# A criterion widened from the item's prose misses what the ADR added

09.5.1 stopped mid-flight because "an empty project" had no valid document, and
the item wrote the three ways out and handed the choice up. The ruling came back
as ADR 26, and the review that followed widened `done_when` from one `-k`
disjunction into three commands — one for OPEN LOCATION, one for the mint, one
for the model's refusal — which is the right repair for
[a -k disjunction is green for the disjunct that names nothing](2026.08.09-a-k-disjunction-is-green-for-the-disjunct-that-names-nothing.md)
and was checked as such.

The three legs were read off the item. But the item's job at that point was to
pose the question, not to state the work: it listed the sites a `None` source
would touch as an argument for how large the change was, and the ADR then made
that list an obligation — "the cost is borne at the readers … each owe a refusal
naming the file". The worker built exactly that, well: one `footage_of` in
`run_cmd` so three commands refuse in one spelling, with the project file in the
message. Nothing asks whether it works. Delete the catch and the suite is
green to the frame.

The failure has no red anywhere by construction. The item's criterion is about
the item; the ADR's clause is in the ADR; the reviewer widening the criterion is
reading the item file, where the clause appears as a parenthetical inside a
rejected-alternatives list. Every artifact is individually correct and the
obligation falls between them.

What distinguishes this from ordinary missing coverage is who was in a position
to notice. The worker satisfied a criterion that named its three claims and
had no reason to read the fourth as owed; the widening review had the ADR in
front of it and derived the legs from the older text. So the check is cheap and
specific: when the ruling an item waited on states a cost, the clause that
states it is a leg, and the item's paragraphs predate the ruling by definition.
