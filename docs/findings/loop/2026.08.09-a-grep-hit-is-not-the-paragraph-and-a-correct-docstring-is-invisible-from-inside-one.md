# A grep hit is not the paragraph, and a correct docstring is invisible from inside one

The two failures look identical from outside — a session mints an item arguing
for something the repo settled days earlier — and they have nothing in common
underneath.

**The first is a reading defect and the documents are blameless.**
`background_ema.py` says, in one paragraph: that it is the tool the admission
rule exists to refuse, that its epsilon is a tolerance and not a bound, that "an
entry is bit-for-bit or it is not an entry", and that what would change the
answer is a measurement of whether a sub-epsilon difference survives into a
detection flip — the measurement the item was eventually rewritten to ask for.
It cites the ADR on the line after the conclusion. A session that read that
paragraph could not have minted the item that was minted. What the session read
was a grep for two symbols, which returned the conclusion without the argument,
and the conclusion alone ("EPSILON is what denies it a key") reads as a
limitation to be worked around rather than as a ruling that considered the
workaround and refused it.

This is the cost `the-admission-argument-is-retold-in-four-modules.md` predicts,
arriving from the opposite direction to the one that item argues. Its case is
that four restatements are three too many; this session's case is that the
restatement was so complete that the citation beside it looked decorative. Both
point at the same remedy — the site states what binds and the ADR holds why —
but the second reason is the stronger one, because a restatement that is
*correct* is exactly the kind a reader stops at.

**The second is a staleness defect and no reader could have caught it.** The
question of what a picked file's meaning is keyed on was reserved inside a
section of an item; that item went `done` with the section still in it; the
offering ruling closed half the question at 03:11 and ADR 29 closed the other
half at 15:53. `pick.py`, written at 07:39 between them, tells its reader the
question is open, and was correct when written. `PLAN.md`'s open-questions list
read "None open" throughout, because it reads item status and not item bodies.
Every document was internally consistent and the tree as a whole was not.

The asymmetry worth keeping is which direction the citation ran. The offering
ruling cites the item whose section held the question, twice. ADR 29 cites it
zero times — and ADR 29 is the one that made the question dead. A ruling that
closes a named open question is the one place a back-reference is load-bearing,
because the question's own file is where the next reader will arrive, and that
file cannot know it has been answered. Forward references are already watched:
the index scans deferrals for a filename and reports the ones whose gate now
reads `done`. Nothing watches the reverse.

What would falsify the reading above: if the session had read lines 36-46 and
minted the item anyway, this would be a defect in the ADR's clarity rather than
in how it was reached, and the remedy would be in the ADR. The transcript says
otherwise — the paragraph was never returned by anything the session ran.
